---

The message box is where you type a request. It sits in the middle of the home screen and at the bottom of every session, and it does three jobs: starting a task, adding to one already running, and answering a question Piper asks.

Piper formats markdown as you type, so `**bold**` renders bold. Piper receives the markdown itself rather than a styled document.

## Dictating instead of typing

Select **Voice input**, the microphone button under the box, and speak. Your words land in the box as you go. Select **Stop recording** to finish.

Dictation runs on your Mac. Piper transcribes your speech with a speech model it downloads once and keeps on the machine, so the audio never leaves it.

<Note>
  **Voice input** stays inactive while that download runs, and its tooltip reads **Voice input is still being set up**. If the download finishes with a session already open, move to another screen and back to use it.
</Note>

<Warning>
  The first time you dictate, your Mac asks for microphone permission. Decline it and Piper cannot record. Allow microphone access for Piper on your Mac, then try **Voice input** again.
</Warning>

## Adding a message while Piper works

You do not have to wait for a turn to end before saying something else. Type while Piper is working and press Enter. Piper holds your message above the box as a card headed **Waiting to send**, and the heading counts them once you queue a second, as **2 waiting to send**.

A waiting card carries two controls. Select **Send now** to hand that message over immediately, steering the work in progress. Select **Remove** to withdraw it. Leave the card alone and it goes on its own when the current turn finishes. Once Piper has taken the message and the current step is still running, the card reads **Delivering when the current step finishes** in place of the two buttons.

The queue is how you correct course. Piper starts down the wrong path, you queue "use the Q3 numbers, not Q2" and select **Send now**, and the work turns without starting over.

<Tip>
  Select **Stop** to halt the current turn outright, rather than queueing a correction behind it.
</Tip>

## How much room the session has

A session's approval mode decides how much Piper may do in it without asking you. Set it from the dropdown in the row under the message box, whose tooltip reads **Session approval mode**. **Manual** stops for your approval whenever the sandbox cannot vouch for an action on its own. **Autopilot** stops asking, so every command and file edit for the rest of that session runs without a prompt, sending included.

A new session starts on **Autopilot**. The exception is a session inside a project you pointed at one of your own folders, which starts on **Manual**, because its work lands outside Piper's own store. [Approvals and permissions](/control/approvals-and-permissions) covers what each mode allows.

<Warning>
  Autopilot does not switch the sandbox off. Anything the sandbox refuses stays refused in either mode, and two actions ask you every time however the session is set: reading a file that holds sign-in credentials, and working in your own signed-in Chrome.
</Warning>

<Note>
  The approval dropdown appears in a session, not on the home screen. Send your first message, then set the mode.
</Note>
