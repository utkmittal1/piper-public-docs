A skill is a folder of written instructions, sometimes with scripts alongside them, teaching Copus how one kind of work is done: building a slide deck, writing a campaign plan, prepping for a sales call. Each skill includes a description of what it's for, and Copus reads those descriptions when a task starts. When your request matches a skill, Copus loads the whole procedure and follows it.

Copus picks the skill for each task. Name the deliverable you want, such as a deck, a dashboard, or a spreadsheet, and Copus finds the skill that builds it. You decide which skills Copus carries in the first place.

This page covers how to:

- [See what each pack covers](#see-what-each-pack-covers), and which pack you start with
- [Switch a skill or a pack on](#switch-a-skill-or-a-pack-on), and what a lock on a row means
- [Add your own skill](#add-your-own-skill), built with Copus or uploaded
- [See the skills Copus always carries](#see-the-skills-copus-always-carries)

## See what each pack covers

Copus groups its switchable skills into six packs. A pack is a themed set of skills for one kind of work.

| Pack | What it covers | Skills inside |
|---|---|---|
| Essentials | Everyday work: meeting minutes, internal updates, decks, and page mockups | 4 |
| Creative | Design and production: page mockups, brand systems, social graphics, logos, and demo videos | 7 |
| Data & Analysis | Querying, validating, charting, dashboards, and financial models | 11 |
| Marketing | Content, campaigns, email sequences, SEO, and performance reporting | 10 |
| Product Management | Specs, roadmaps, research synthesis, sprints, and stakeholder updates | 8 |
| Sales | Account research, call prep, outreach, pipeline review, and forecasting | 9 |

Two skills sit in two packs each. **Design a Page or Prototype** belongs to Essentials and Creative. **Competitive Brief** belongs to Marketing and Product Management.

Each skill covers one narrow job. With **Create Slides**, Copus builds a presentation deck as a web deck or an editable PowerPoint file. With **Write a Query**, Copus turns a plain-English question into an optimized SQL query for your database. With **Build a Dashboard**, Copus assembles an interactive dashboard with filters, KPI cards, and tables. With **Prep for a Call**, Copus works out who is on the call, what they care about, and what to ask.

### Which pack you start with

Copus starts with **Essentials** switched on and every other pack switched off. Switch on more packs or single skills on the **Skills** page in the sidebar whenever you need them.

## Switch a skill or a pack on

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the list">
    Select **Skills** in the sidebar. Each pack is a card with a count of how many of its skills are on, such as `4/11`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Switch a whole pack">
    Use the switch on the pack card to turn every skill in that pack on. Switch the pack off to turn them all back off, with two exceptions. A skill that a second pack also holds stays on if every skill in that second pack is on. A skill that some other switched-on skill needs stays on too.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick individual skills">
    Select a pack card to open it, then use the checkbox on any row. Hover the information icon beside a name to read **Loads when:** and the description Copus matches your request against.
  </Step>
  <Step title="See what a pack pairs with">
    An open pack ends with **Works best with**, naming the apps its skills draw on, such as **Slack**, **Gmail**, **Google Drive**, and **Google Calendar** for Essentials. Hover an app's name to see why the pack uses it. Set an app up on the [Integrations](/connections/integrations) page. **Amplitude**, **SimilarWeb**, and **CRM (HubSpot / Salesforce)** aren't in Copus's integrations catalog.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A row with a lock beside its name has a ticked checkbox you can't clear, because another switched-on skill needs that skill. Hover the row and Copus names the skill to switch off first, for example **Needed by Make a Demo Video. Switch that off first.**

When you switch a skill on, Copus also switches on every skill that skill needs, without telling you. That's why a count can jump by more than one. **Make a Demo Video** is the shipped skill that needs other skills today. Switch it on and Copus also switches on **Design a Page or Prototype** and **Plan a Demo**, so the Creative count moves by three.

<Tip>
A skill can also carry a note about something to arrange outside Copus. When you switch such a skill on, Copus opens a dialog with the skill's name and the line **Before you use this skill**, saying what to arrange. Copus switches the skill on whether or not you arrange it, and doesn't check. **Make a Demo Video** carries a note today, asking for an ElevenLabs API key saved in **Secrets**.
</Tip>

## Add your own skill

Copus can build a skill with you, or you can upload a skill somebody sends you. Both start on the **Skills** page in the sidebar.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Build a skill with Copus">
    Select **Create skill**. Answer **What should this skill do?** and **When should it load?**, then select **Start building**. Copus starts only when you've answered both. Copus then opens a task and works through the skill with you, so set up an AI provider first.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the result to your library">
    When the task finishes, a card reads **Skill ready. Add it to your library?**. Copus adds the skill only after you select **Add to library**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Or upload a package">
    Select **Upload skill**, read the warning, then **Choose file…** and pick the `.zip`. A skill package is a `.zip` of the skill's folder, holding a file called `SKILL.md`, which is the skill's instructions written in plain text. When you pick the file, you accept the warning.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find your skills again">
    Copus lists the skills you added under **Custom Skills**, each marked `Created` or `Uploaded`. Hover a row for the delete button.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Copus switches on a skill you add straight away. Copus refuses a `.zip` that is too large or holds too many files, and tells you which limit it hit.

<Warning>
A skill's instructions and any scripts bundled with it run with the same access Copus has. Only add skills from people and sources you trust.
</Warning>

<Note>
When you delete a custom skill, Copus removes it, and you can't undo the deletion. Copus refuses the deletion while another skill depends on that skill. A built-in skill has only a checkbox, so you can switch it off but can't delete it.
</Note>

## See the skills Copus always carries

Copus also carries a set of its own skills that you won't find on the **Skills** page. They cover reading and writing Word files, PDFs, spreadsheets, and presentations; writing in plain, clear English; drawing charts and tables inside a reply; using a stored credential; building a new skill; setting up a scheduled task; and answering your questions about Copus itself. Copus carries them in every task, and you can't switch them off.
