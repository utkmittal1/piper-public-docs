Piper ships with two themes: **Moss**, the light one, and **Dusk**, the dark one. Your choice sets the colours of the window, the cards in a reply, and the charts and diagrams Piper draws.

## The two themes

| Theme | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Moss** | The light theme. Green-grey paper, near-black green text, and a rust accent. |
| **Dusk** | The dark theme. Deep green-charcoal with a soft sage glow at the top and bottom of the window, and the same rust accent. |

A fresh install starts on **Moss**. There are exactly two themes, and no way to add a third.

## Switching theme

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the user menu">
    Select the row with your name at the bottom of the sidebar. If the sidebar is collapsed, expand it first with the panel button at the top.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick a theme">
    Under **Theme**, select **Moss** or **Dusk**. Each option shows a dot in that theme's paper colour, ringed in the accent.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The whole window changes as you select, with nothing to save and nothing to reload. Piper remembers your choice, so reopening Piper puts you straight back in the theme you picked. The same menu holds your profile and the AI provider that does Piper's thinking.

## What the theme covers

The theme reaches further than the window frame. The charts and diagrams Piper draws inside a reply carry their own set of series colours per theme, so a bar chart in **Dusk** comes back in a brighter, more pastel palette than the same chart in **Moss**, which runs earthy and rust-anchored. Cards, tables, dashboards Piper builds into a reply, and the file viewer follow the theme too.

Finished files keep their own styling instead. A deck or a document Piper writes for you is a real file, and it looks the same whichever theme Piper is in. A spreadsheet works both ways: the file keeps its own formatting, and Piper draws its preview as a plain grid in your theme. [What Piper hands back](/working/files-and-deliverables) covers the viewer and the file card.

## Limits on the theme

<Warning>
Piper does not follow your Mac's light or dark setting. Your theme choice is Piper's own, and it stays where you put it whatever macOS does at sunset. If you want Piper dark in the evening, switch it yourself.
</Warning>

<Note>
Piper does follow one macOS setting. Turn Reduce Motion on and Piper draws still frames in place of its animations.
</Note>

<Tip>
The theme is per Mac, not per account. Piper stores the choice on the machine you set it on, so a second Mac starts on **Moss** again.
</Tip>

<Note>
A slide deck has its own theme, unrelated to this one. Ask Piper for a deck and you pick from a grid of theme cards, which sets the deck's colours and fonts rather than Piper's own. Piper draws each card from the same theme file it later reads to build the deck, so what you tap is what you get. [Skills](/working/skills) covers how Piper builds decks.
</Note>

## Collapsing the sidebar

Collapse the sidebar when you want the window to itself. Select the panel button at its top edge, labelled **Collapse sidebar**, and Piper hides the session list, the bell, the settings gear, and the user menu, along with the wordmark and the back and forward arrows. The nav items stay as icons without their labels. Select **Expand sidebar** to bring it all back, and Piper remembers which way you left it.

Expand it again to reach the theme switcher or Settings. Both live in the part that hides, and no keyboard shortcut opens either. Escape closes Settings once you are in it.
