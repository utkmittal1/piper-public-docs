Copus draws in one of two themes: **Moss**, the light theme, and **Dusk**, the dark theme. You can also choose **System**, and Copus follows your Mac's own light or dark setting, using Moss or Dusk to match. Copus starts on **System** after a fresh install.

| Choice | What Copus does |
|---|---|
| **System** | Follows your Mac, and changes with it while Copus is open |
| **Moss** | Stays in the light theme, whatever your Mac is set to |
| **Dusk** | Stays in the dark theme, whatever your Mac is set to |

This page covers how to:

- [Pick a theme](#pick-a-theme)
- [See what the theme changes](#see-what-the-theme-changes), inside a reply as well as around it
- [See what keeps its own colors](#see-what-keeps-its-own-colors), finished files included
- [Pick a theme for a slide deck](#pick-a-theme-for-a-slide-deck), which is a separate choice

## Pick a theme

Open the user menu, the chip at the foot of the sidebar with your name on it, or **Set up profile** if you haven't filled your profile in. Under **Theme**, select **System**, **Moss**, or **Dusk**.

Copus changes the whole window as soon as you select a theme. Copus applies your theme before it draws the first frame, so Copus opens in your theme when you reopen it. The same menu holds your profile and **AI Providers**.

<Tip>
  Copus stores your theme on the Mac you set it on. On a second Mac, Copus starts on **System**.
</Tip>

## See what the theme changes

Copus draws the charts and diagrams inside a reply from a set of series colors that belongs to the theme. The eight colors in **Moss** are earthy and anchored on its rust accent. The eight in **Dusk** are brighter and more pastel, so they stay legible against a near-black background. Copus redraws a chart when you switch theme. Copus also draws cards, tables, the dashboards it builds into a reply, and the file viewer in your theme.

<Note>
  Copus follows one macOS setting besides light and dark. Turn Reduce Motion on and Copus draws a still frame in place of each of its animations. Copus applies the change at once.
</Note>

## See what keeps its own colors

Copus shows a deck it wrote in the slides' own colors in either theme, because those colors belong to the file.

Copus's preview of a Word document or a spreadsheet uses your theme in place of the file's formatting. The file on disk keeps its own formatting, and you see it when you open the file in Word or Excel. Copus draws the structure of a Word document, its headings and paragraphs and lists, on a page in your theme. Copus draws a spreadsheet as a plain grid in your theme. In both previews, Copus leaves out the fonts and fills of the original.

Copus opens a saved HTML page on its own white canvas, the way a browser shows it. [What Copus hands back](/working/files-and-deliverables) covers the viewer and the file card.

## Pick a theme for a slide deck

A slide deck has a theme of its own, separate from Copus's. Ask Copus for a deck and Copus asks you two questions first: whether you want a web deck or an editable PowerPoint file, and where the deck's look should come from. Answer the second question with a built-in theme and Copus offers a grid of theme cards under the question **Which look?**. Eight themes ship: Copus, Boardroom, Signal, Mist, Meadow, Noir, Harbor, and Orchid. The theme you pick sets the deck's colors and fonts. Copus's own theme stays as it is.

Attach a template of your own, or a screenshot of a deck to match, and Copus skips the theme cards. Copus builds the deck from what you gave it.

Copus draws each theme card from the same file it later reads to build the deck, so the deck looks like the card you selected. [Skills](/working/skills) covers how Copus builds decks.
