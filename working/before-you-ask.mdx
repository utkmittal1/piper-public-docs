Piper reads the apps you connect, finds the work that's waiting on you, and does it before you ask. It produces the documents, analyses, and research people ask you for, writes prep documents for your meetings, drafts replies that need thought, and suggests follow-ups on conversations that have stalled. Each result waits on the Home page until you review it.

This page covers how Piper decides what to take on, what it hands back, and how to review, refine, or stop it. For tasks you start yourself, see [How Piper works](/concepts/how-piper-works).

## How it works

Every 15 minutes, Piper reads what changed in your connected apps and decides which threads and meetings ask something of you. For each thread or meeting that does, Piper creates a task, does the work, and puts the result on the Home page. You review the result, ask for changes, and decide what happens next.

For example, when a colleague emails to ask for a comparison of two vendors by Friday, Piper might:

1. Read the thread and note the request and the deadline.
2. Suggest the comparison as an idea on the Home page, because the work is large.
3. Research the two vendors after you select **Do it**.
4. Write the comparison as an analysis with charts.
5. Put the analysis on the Home page and notify you.

You read the analysis, ask for a change with **Refine** if something is off, and select **Done** when you finish with it.

Piper follows the same steps for every other kind of result: a document someone requests from you, a prep document for an upcoming meeting, a reply that needs thought, a proposed calendar event, or a set of choices when a decision is yours to make.

Before its first task, Piper builds your People list from your sent mail. While Piper works on its first tasks, it writes a guide to how you write. Every week, Piper updates both from what you do with its results.

## Apps Piper works from

Piper reads every supported app you connect. Piper finds work in Gmail and Google Calendar. Other apps give Piper context while it works.

| App | What Piper does with it |
|---|---|
| Gmail | Reads every thread that changes, except mail Gmail files under Promotions, Social, or Forums, and starts or suggests a task when a thread asks something of you |
| Google Calendar | Reads your upcoming meetings and writes a prep document for each meeting that needs one. Also shows your meetings in the **Coming up** box on the Home page, and checks each proposed event for clashes |
| Google Drive | Gives Piper access to your files while it works on a task |

## Requirements

- You're signed in to an [AI provider](/concepts/ai-providers).
- Gmail or Google Calendar is connected.
- Piper is open, and your Mac is awake.

## Connect your apps

Piper starts reading your mail as soon as you connect Gmail. You can connect your Google apps from two places:

- **Connect Google**, during setup or on the Home page. One Google sign-in connects Gmail, Google Calendar, and Google Drive. The connection fails if you leave any of the three apps' permissions unapproved.
- **Settings** > **Integrations**. Each Google app connects separately, with its own sign-in.

If you skip Google during setup, you can connect it later from either place.

### What Piper reads first

When you first connect Gmail, Piper reads your mailbox once to learn about you:

1. Piper reads your recent sent mail and builds your People list. The people you write to most, and most recently, rank highest.
2. Piper reads your recent threads with the people at the top of your People list, and creates its first tasks from them. At the same time, Piper writes a guide to how you write and saves facts about you to your [memory](/context/memory).

To see and edit what Piper learned, see [What Piper learns about you](/context/what-piper-learns).

## What Piper takes on

Piper creates a task when a thread asks for thought or effort from you:

- A reply that needs thought, such as a decision you have to answer with care
- A document, analysis, comparison, or research someone requests
- A conversation where you're waiting on someone, or someone is waiting on you
- A question only you can answer, such as a yes or no, a date, or a preference

Piper skips notifications, receipts, newsletters, replies you'd write in seconds, and mail from strangers, unless the thread clearly needs your action.

Piper starts small, clear tasks right away. When a task is large or its scope is unclear, Piper suggests the task as an idea instead. Piper starts the idea when you select **Do it**, or when new mail on the idea's thread changes the work. Piper also suggests work nobody asked for, such as a summary of where a project stands, but only from conversations with the people at the top of your People list. That kind of work always arrives as an idea.

Once a day, Piper looks for Gmail threads where nobody has replied for three days or more. When someone is waiting on you, Piper starts the reply or suggests it. When you're waiting on someone, Piper usually suggests a short follow-up for you to send. Piper leaves a quiet thread alone when the silence has a reason, such as a settled matter.

Piper also reads your upcoming meetings in Google Calendar. For a meeting that needs preparation, Piper writes a prep document and shows it next to the meeting in the **Coming up** box on the Home page.

While a task is open, new mail on its thread goes to that task. When the new message changes what the task needs, Piper works on the task again with the new message in front of it. New mail on the thread of a closed task starts a new task.

## What you get back

When Piper finishes a task, the task on the Home page holds what Piper produced. A task can hold more than one result, such as an analysis and a reply to send with the analysis.

| Result | What Piper hands you |
|---|---|
| Email draft | A reply to the thread in your writing style, with the recipients and subject filled in. |
| Analysis | A written answer to the request in the thread, with charts. When the thread needs a response, Piper adds a reply for you to send. |
| Meeting prep | A prep document for an upcoming meeting. Piper shows the document next to the meeting in the **Coming up** box on the Home page. |
| Calendar event | A proposed event with the title, time, and guests filled in. Piper lists any meeting already on your calendar at that time. |
| File | A file Piper made for the task, such as a presentation, spreadsheet, PDF, Word document, or image. |
| Question | A decision only you can make, with two or three choices. Each choice holds the reply or event Piper prepared for that answer. |

Two kinds of task wait on you before Piper produces a result:

- **Idea**: Piper suggests the task and hasn't started it. The task shows why Piper suggests it and what you get from it.
- **Stuck**: Piper stopped because it needs something from you. The task names what Piper needs.

Piper notifies you when a result is ready, when a task needs your answer, and when a task is stuck. To change these alerts, see [Notifications](/working/notifications).

## Review and respond

On each result, you select the action that fits it, such as **Send**, **Create event**, or **Done**. On an idea, you select **Do it** to start the task or **Decline** to turn it down. Two actions also shape what Piper does next:

- **Refine**: you tell Piper in one line what to change, and Piper reworks the same task.
- **Dismiss**: Piper closes the task and records your reason: **I handled it myself**, **Not something I want Piper to work on**, or **Didn’t do it well**. The weekly review reads these reasons.

To answer a question with something other than the choices offered, select **Something else**, describe what you want, and select **Prepare reply**. To unblock a stuck task, type the missing detail into **Tell Piper what it needs to carry on**, then select **Refine**.

The Home page shows your most urgent tasks first. To see every open task, select the line below the list, such as "Four more", to open the **Piper's tasks** list. To see each step Piper takes on a task, select **Open transcript**.

Piper closes a task tied to a time, such as meeting prep, once that time passes.

## How Piper learns from your responses

Piper records what you do with each result: send it unchanged, edit it before sending, refine it, dismiss it, or decline it. Once a week, Piper reviews the week and updates:

- **Your memory**, with facts under **Proactive help** about the work you want and the work you don't
- **Your writing guide**, where your edits show a habit the guide doesn't describe yet
- **Your People list**, with notes on the people in that week's tasks

Piper saves a fact when it sees a pattern across your tasks. You find your writing guide on the **How you write** tab of the **About you** page, next to the **People** and **Memory** tabs. To review or edit what Piper saved, see [What Piper learns about you](/context/what-piper-learns).

## When Piper checks

| Schedule | What Piper does |
|---|---|
| Every 15 minutes | Reads what changed in Gmail since the last check |
| Daily | Looks at quiet Gmail threads, and starts or suggests replies and follow-ups |
| Weekly | Reviews the week's results and updates your memory, writing guide, and People list |

## Stop Piper from starting tasks

To stop Piper from starting tasks from an app, open **Settings** > **Integrations**, select Gmail or Google Calendar, and disconnect the app. Piper also loses access to that app in tasks you start.

## Limitations

- **Piper finds work in Gmail and Google Calendar only.** Google Drive and your other connected apps support the tasks Piper starts.
- **Piper skips three Gmail tabs.** Mail that Gmail files under Promotions, Social, or Forums never becomes a task, including mailing-list mail that lands in Forums.
- **Piper works only while it's open.** Checks run only while Piper is open and your Mac is awake. When you open Piper after a missed check, Piper runs that check once. If your Google sign-in expires, the Home page shows a message, and checks stop until you connect Google again in **Settings** > **Integrations**.
- **Piper can misjudge a thread.** Piper sometimes takes on a thread you'd ignore, or misses a thread you want handled. The weekly review reads every task you dismiss or decline.
- **Gmail and Google Calendar stay read-only in tasks Piper starts.** You send every message with **Send** and create every event with **Create event**. In your other connected apps, Piper uses every tool switched on for that app in **Settings** > **Integrations**. In tasks Piper starts, a tool set to **Ask** runs without asking you first.
- **Piper writes only to people it knows.** A reply can go only to someone on a thread Piper read, or to someone in your People list.
- **Your mail goes to your AI provider.** To decide what needs work and to do it, Piper sends mail to your AI provider. The first read sends your recent sent mail and your recent threads with the people at the top of your People list. Each 15-minute check sends every changed thread outside the Promotions, Social, and Forums tabs in full, with Piper's notes on the people in it, and contacts your provider only when a thread has changed. The daily check sends the quiet threads it looks at. See [Your data stays local](/control/your-data-stays-local).
