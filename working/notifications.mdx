A notification tells you that work has finished, that something needs your decision, or that something has broken. Piper raises nine kinds of notification, each with a switch of its own, and every kind is on until you switch it off.

This page covers how to:

- [See what each notification tells you](#see-what-each-notification-tells-you)
- [Find where a notification reaches you](#find-where-a-notification-reaches-you), from the bell to a macOS banner
- [Switch a notification off](#switch-a-notification-off), and empty the bell
- [Turn on macOS banners](#turn-on-macos-banners), and fix a banner that never arrives
- [Set your notification hours](#set-your-notification-hours)
- [See when Piper skips a notification](#see-when-piper-skips-a-notification)

## See what each notification tells you

| Notification | When it fires |
|---|---|
| Agent responded | When Piper finishes responding somewhere you're not watching. |
| Approval needed | When Piper needs your go-ahead to run a tool. |
| Connector broken | When a connected tool stops working. |
| Run could not finish | When a run of a scheduled task stops before it delivers. |
| Task stopped itself | When a task stops on its own, after runs fail or a job goes quiet. |
| Job finished | When a multi-step task finishes everything it was set. |
| Ready for you | When a task Piper started is ready to review on the Home page. |
| Piper needs you | When a task Piper started is waiting on your answer or is stuck. |
| Memory cleanup suggested | When memory is getting long and Piper has cleanup suggestions to review. |

A scheduled task raises a notification only when a run can't finish or the task stops itself. A run that delivers raises nothing.

When you select a notification, Piper opens what the notification is about:

- **Agent responded** and **Approval needed**: the conversation.
- **Run could not finish**, **Task stopped itself**, and **Job finished**: the task.
- **Connector broken**: **Settings** > **Integrations**.
- **Ready for you** and **Piper needs you**: the Home page, with that task open beside it.
- **Memory cleanup suggested**: **About you** > **Memory**, or the project or scheduled task whose memory is filling up.

## Find where a notification reaches you

### The bell

The bell sits at the foot of the sidebar, with a badge that counts what arrived since you last opened it, up to `9+`. When you open the bell's **Notifications** panel, Piper clears the count. Piper keeps each notification in the bell until you dismiss it, until it reaches a set age, or until newer notifications push it out.

### Dots in the sidebar

For **Agent responded** and **Approval needed**, Piper marks the conversation in the sidebar with a dot instead of adding a row to the bell. Piper clears the dot when you open the conversation.

### Popups

Piper shows a popup card in the top right corner as a notification arrives, and closes the card after a few seconds. To keep a card open, hover over it. When a notification has somewhere to send you, its card shows **View**.

### macOS banners

A banner reaches you outside Piper's window. Banners start switched off. Piper posts a banner only while no Piper window has focus.

### Sound

Piper plays a short chime when a notification arrives, while **Sound** is on.

## Switch a notification off

Open **Settings** > **Notifications**. The **Events** section lists all nine kinds, each with a switch. The **Delivery** section holds **In-app**, **Sound**, and **macOS banners**. **Sound** and **macOS banners** have switches, and **In-app** reads **Always on**.

When you switch off a row in **Events**, Piper stops raising that kind everywhere: in the bell, in a popup, in a banner, and in the chime. Notifications of that kind already in the bell stay there.

To empty the bell, open it and select the × on a row, or select **Clear all**. Piper deletes each notification you dismiss.

## Turn on macOS banners

<Steps>
  <Step title="Switch banners on">
    Open **Settings** > **Notifications** and switch on **macOS banners**. Piper sends a test banner at once, even while Piper has focus, and shows a popup that says whether the test banner went out.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fix a banner that never came">
    If the test banner didn't appear, select **Open settings**, in that popup or in the **Not showing?** row under the switch. In the macOS notification settings, allow notifications for Piper and set its style to Banners or Alerts.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  macOS decides whether a banner appears. Piper posts the banner and opens the right macOS settings pane. If macOS holds notifications for Piper, no banner appears.
</Note>

## Set your notification hours

Piper starts with notification hours already set to the day and evening. A notification raised outside your hours waits, then arrives once when the hours open.

To change your hours, open **Settings** > **Notifications** and pick the times under **Notification hours**, in **Between** … **and**. To cover the night, pick an end time earlier than the start time. To receive notifications at any hour, pick the same start and end time. Notification hours apply to every kind of notification.

## See when Piper skips a notification

Piper skips **Agent responded** and **Approval needed** while their conversation is on screen and Piper has focus. Piper skips **Ready for you** and **Piper needs you** while the Home page is on screen and Piper has focus. Piper drops a skipped notification, because you already have the conversation or the Home page in front of you. After you switch to another app, Piper raises these notifications as normal.

<Note>
  You answer an approval in the conversation that raised it. The **Approval needed** notification opens that conversation. [Approvals and permissions](/control/approvals-and-permissions) covers what Piper asks and how to answer. Work Piper starts waits on the Home page. See [Work Piper starts before you ask](/working/before-you-ask).
</Note>
