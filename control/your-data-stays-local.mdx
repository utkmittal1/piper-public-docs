Piper keeps your tasks, files, memory, and credentials on your Mac, in a folder you can open, back up, and delete. Piper still sends requests to your AI provider over the network, with your conversation and the content of any file a task read. This page explains what stays on your Mac and what Piper sends out.

This page covers how to:

- [Find where Piper keeps your data](#where-piper-keeps-your-data), folder by folder
- [Read what the log file records](#what-the-log-file-records), including the part that holds your own words
- [See what the database holds](#what-the-database-holds), and a credential that can land in it
- [Find where your credentials live](#where-your-credentials-live), and the sign-ins kept in a file
- [Learn what leaves your Mac](#what-leaves-your-mac), and why each route exists
- [Know what Piper downloads on first launch](#what-piper-downloads-on-first-launch)

## Where Piper keeps your data

Piper keeps what it manages in one folder called `PiperData`, inside your home folder. You can open it in Finder. The database file `piper.db` sits at the top of it.

| Folder | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `sessions/` | The workspace of every conversation that doesn't belong to a project, including every task Piper starts on its own |
| `projects/` | Project folders, and the folder of every scheduled and long-running task |
| `logs/` | One log file, `piper.jsonl` |
| `knowledge/models/` | The model Piper indexes your documents with |
| `speech/models/` | The model Piper transcribes dictation with |
| `runtime/` | The Python Piper runs scripts with, its own browser, and the logins for sites you signed in to |
| `skills/` | Skills you added yourself |
| `.claude/` and `.codex/` | What Claude Code and Codex write as they run, including their sign-ins |

For a project you pointed at a folder of your own, Piper keeps that project's files in a subfolder it creates inside your folder, and every task in the project writes inside that subfolder. See [Projects](/concepts/projects) for the layout, and [Tasks and the workspace](/concepts/sessions-and-workspace) for what a task's own folder holds.

Piper builds the search index for your Knowledge Sources on this machine. It reads your documents, splits them into passages, and indexes them with a model it downloads once and runs here. Piper also searches that index on your Mac. See [Knowledge](/context/knowledge-sources).

### What the log file records

Piper keeps recent log lines in `PiperData/logs/piper.jsonl` and deletes older lines on its own. To open the log, go to **Settings** and select **Logs**.

Piper writes one line for each action it takes, with a timestamp and details such as the task and project the action belongs to, the AI provider that ran it, a count, a duration, or the message of an error.

Two kinds of line carry your own words. When a turn starts, Piper logs the start of the message that started it. When Piper removes or rewords a memory fact during cleanup, it logs that fact's full text. Piper keeps its own replies and the contents of the files a task read out of the log, and records a reply only as a length.

## What the database holds

`piper.db` holds your tasks and their transcripts, your memory facts, your settings, which integrations you have connected, and the indexed text of your Knowledge Sources. It also holds what Piper learns from your mail: your People list, with each person's name, addresses, and notes, and your guide to how you write, with earlier versions of both. See [What Piper learns about you](/context/what-piper-learns).

For a password, API key, or access token you saved under **Secrets**, and for an integration you connected, Piper stores only the name of each field. Piper saves the value itself in your keychain.

<Warning>
  Piper can store one kind of credential in the database. If you add an MCP server of your own that runs on this machine, Piper stores the environment variables you gave it exactly as you typed them, so a token typed into that box is kept in `piper.db` in plain text. Save the token under **Secrets** instead and point the server at the secret.
</Warning>

## Where your credentials live

Piper saves every password, API key, and access token you save under **Secrets** into your Mac's own keychain, and passes the value only to the one command that needs it. If the keychain refuses a value, Piper fails the save and keeps no plain-text copy. Piper stores the credential behind every integration you connect the same way. See [Secrets](/connections/secrets).

Claude Code and Codex keep their sign-ins in files instead, both inside `PiperData`.

**Claude Code** writes its token to `.claude/.credentials.json`.

**Codex** writes its token to `.codex/auth.json`, because the Codex program reads its tokens from that file rather than from the keychain.

Piper asks you before a task reads either file, every time, in every approval mode, and waits for your answer. In work Piper runs with nobody watching, including every task Piper starts on its own, Piper refuses the read instead of asking. See [Approvals and permissions](/control/approvals-and-permissions) for both rules.

## What leaves your Mac

**To the AI provider.** The provider you pick in **Settings** under **AI Providers** runs on its own machines. On every turn, Piper sends the provider your prompt, the conversation so far, your memory, your instructions, and the text of any file a task read. Piper adds whatever a task reads to the conversation, so the text of that file leaves your Mac. When you save an API key, Piper sends that provider one more request, to list the models your account can reach.

**To the AI provider, for work Piper starts on its own.** Once you connect Gmail, Piper sends your AI provider the mail threads it reads to find and do work, with its notes on the people in each thread. When Piper first reads your mailbox, it also sends your recent sent mail. To see what each check sends, see [Work Piper starts before you ask](/working/before-you-ask#limitations).

**To an integration's own service.** Piper sends the service only what the action needs. To read a Slack channel, Piper sends Slack the channel to read. To send an email through Gmail, Piper sends Gmail the recipients, the subject, and the body. Piper uses each integration to reach only that integration's own service. Piper reaches an MCP server you add yourself at the address you gave it.

**To Google, when you connect Google.** When you select **Connect Google** during setup or on the Home page, Piper asks Google for your name and email address, and saves them to your profile.

**To Piper's sign-in relay, if you use Piper's built-in sign-in.** When you connect Gmail, Google Calendar, Google Drive, or Slack through Piper's built-in sign-in, Piper sends the sign-in, and every later renewal of its access token, through Piper's relay at get-piper.com, which passes the request on to Google or Slack. When you connect with an app of your own, Piper talks to Google or Slack directly. See [Integrations](/connections/integrations).

**To the sites Piper opens.** When Piper opens a page, Piper sends that site the page address and your network address, the same as your own browser does. Piper can also click and fill in a page in its browser, so anything Piper types into a form goes to that site.

**To a web search service.** On Claude Code or Codex, the provider runs the search on its own machines, as part of the same request. On a model API, Piper searches through Brave, and sends your search terms there with the key you added under **Secrets**.

**To Google, for site icons.** When Piper shows you the sources behind a web search, it fetches each site's icon from Google. Piper sends Google the domain of every site listed, whichever AI provider you're on.

**To your iPhone, if you paired an iPhone.** Turn on **Allow iPhone access** under **Settings** > **iPhone**, and Piper serves your name, your tasks, their transcripts and files, your projects, the tasks and meetings on your Home page, and your notifications to that phone over your own Tailscale network. Piper serves data to a phone only while **Allow iPhone access** is on. See [Piper on your iPhone](/working/iphone).

A command can also send your data off your Mac. The sandbox confines only what a task writes, so a command that runs can upload a file of yours anywhere on the internet. Piper controls that by asking before every command outside its read-only list, which is why you can't set **Run commands** to run on its own. On **Autopilot**, Piper answers that question for you. See [Approvals and permissions](/control/approvals-and-permissions).

<Warning>
  "Local" describes where Piper keeps your data at rest. Every request to an AI provider leaves your Mac, whichever provider you choose, because each provider runs on its own machines. If a document is sensitive enough that its text must never reach an AI provider, keep it out of the task.
</Warning>

<Note>
  Piper collects no usage data, analytics, or crash reports, and it doesn't check for its own updates.
</Note>

## What Piper downloads on first launch

Piper fetches these files the first time it starts, then runs them from `PiperData`. For each download, Piper requests a file and sends nothing of yours.

- A copy of Python, from GitHub.
- The Python packages the built-in skills need, from PyPI.
- The Node packages a few other built-in skills need, from the npm registry.
- Its own browser and the program that drives it, from the npm registry and from Playwright.
- The model that indexes your documents, from Hugging Face.
- The model that transcribes your dictation, from Hugging Face.

## Remove what Piper keeps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Remove a single memory fact">
    Open **About you** in the sidebar, select **Memory**, find the fact, and select **Remove fact**. Piper stops applying it to future tasks.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Delete a task">
    Delete the task from the sidebar. Tick **Also delete the folder from disk** to remove its files as well, or leave it unticked and the folder stays where it is.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Remove a credential">
    In **Settings**, select **Secrets**, then **Delete secret** on the entry and confirm with **Delete**. Piper removes the value from your keychain first, then the entry.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the folder yourself">
    In a task's right-hand rail, select **Reveal in Finder** on a file row, or **Open folder in Finder** on the **Other files** heading. From there you can browse, copy, or move whatever you like.
  </Step>
</Steps>

When you delete a task, Piper removes it from its database. Anything Piper already did through an integration stays done: a message Piper sent through Gmail or Slack stays sent, and a file it wrote outside its own folder stays where it landed.

<Tip>
  `PiperData` is an ordinary folder in your home directory, so any backup of your home folder includes it.
</Tip>
