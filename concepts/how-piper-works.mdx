Piper starts work from what it finds in the apps you connect. Piper also runs the tasks you type into the message box on the Home page. Both kinds of task use what Piper knows about you and the apps you connect.

This page covers how both kinds of task run, what Piper reads while it works, and what you control.

## Compare the two ways work starts

| | Work Piper starts | Work you start |
|---|---|---|
| Where it starts | A Gmail thread or a meeting on your Google Calendar | The message box on the Home page |
| How Piper scopes it | Piper reads the thread and decides what the work is. | Piper answers a quick request straight away. For larger work, Piper asks you what it needs to know, then plans the steps. |
| Where the result appears | On the Home page, as a task for you to review | In the conversation, with finished files under **Artifacts** |
| What Piper sends | Piper sends a reply only when you select **Send**, and creates an event only when you select **Create event**. | A new task asks you first. By default, Piper asks before it sends, posts, or changes anything in a connected app, and before it runs a command that changes something on your Mac. You can let a task do these without asking. |

## How Piper starts work on its own

Every 15 minutes, Piper reads what changed in Gmail, reads your upcoming meetings, and starts the work they ask of you. That work includes documents, analyses, research, meeting prep, replies, follow-ups, and decisions laid out as choices. Piper learns from what you do with each result. Each result waits on the Home page for you to review, refine, send, or dismiss.

[Work Piper starts before you ask](/working/before-you-ask) covers what Piper takes on, what it hands back, and how to review or stop it.

## How a task you start runs

A task you start runs in four stages.

**You describe.** Type what you want in the message box on the Home page, and name the outcome rather than the first step. "Pull the Q3 numbers into a one-page summary for Thursday" gets further than "open the spreadsheet." Piper opens a conversation for the task. See [The message box](/working/describing-what-you-need).

**Piper plans.** Piper answers a quick request straight away. For larger work, Piper first asks the questions whose answers change the result, such as the goal, the audience, or the format. You answer them on one card. Piper then posts its steps as a card headed **Execution plan**.

To review the plan before Piper acts on it, switch **Planning mode** on in the task's top bar. Piper writes the plan and waits. Select **Go ahead** to start. To change the plan, type the change, and Piper revises the plan and waits again.

**Piper works.** Piper works through the steps in order. It reads files, searches the web and your documents, uses the tools and apps you have switched on, and writes the output. A strip above the message box names the step running now.

**You review.** Piper replies with charts, sortable tables, diagrams, images, and files that open beside the conversation. Piper writes Word documents, PowerPoint decks, spreadsheets with live formulas, and PDFs, and lists finished files under **Artifacts**. To correct the work, reply in the same conversation. Piper keeps your files, the plan, and your answers from the earlier round.

## What Piper reads while it works

Every task you start reads the following:

- **Memory**: the facts Piper keeps about you between tasks, and the rules Piper follows on every message. Piper builds part of your memory from your mail and from what you do with its work. See [Memory](/context/memory) and [What Piper learns about you](/context/what-piper-learns).
- **Instructions and profile**: your name, your about-me note, and the standing instructions you write for every task, for a project, or for one conversation. See [Instructions and profile](/context/instructions-and-profile).
- **Response style**: the length, tone, formatting, and emoji you set for Piper's replies. See [Response style](/context/response-style).
- **Your documents**: the folders you add as knowledge sources. Piper searches them before it asks you questions. See [Knowledge sources](/context/knowledge-sources).
- **Your apps and the web**: the apps you connect, and the pages Piper reads in its own browser. See [Integrations](/connections/integrations) and [Web and sign-ins](/connections/web-and-sign-ins).
- **Skills**: written instructions for one kind of work, such as building a slide deck. Piper loads the skill that matches the task. See [Skills](/working/skills).

## What the AI provider receives

Piper runs each task on the AI provider you sign in to: Claude Code, Codex, or a model you reach with an API key from Anthropic, OpenAI, or DeepSeek. See [AI providers](/concepts/ai-providers).

Piper stores your files, projects, knowledge sources, memory, People list, and writing guide on your Mac. Piper sends the AI provider only what a task uses:

- The conversation, and each file or document the task reads
- Your memory facts and standing instructions, when a conversation starts and again when they change
- Your memory rules, with every message
- For work Piper starts on its own, the mail threads Piper reads, with Piper's notes on the people in them

See [Your data stays local](/control/your-data-stays-local).

## Choose how much Piper does without asking

A task you start has an approval mode: **Manual** or **Autopilot**. You switch the mode in the controls under the message box. In **Manual**, Piper asks you by default before it sends, posts, or changes anything in a connected app, and before it runs a command that changes something on your Mac. In **Autopilot**, Piper does these without asking for the rest of the conversation. A new task starts in **Manual** unless you change **New tasks start in** under **Settings** > **Built-in tools**.

<Warning>
  On **Autopilot**, Piper can post a message or change a record in a connected app without showing it to you first. Use **Manual** for any task that might post a message or change a record. Email, calendar changes, and Drive sharing and comments are the exception. In both modes, Piper shows you a card for those first, and nothing goes out until you select the button on it.
</Warning>

Work Piper starts on its own runs without asking you. In that work, Gmail and Google Calendar are read-only, so you send every reply and create every event yourself. In your other connected apps, Piper uses every tool switched on for that app in **Settings** > **Integrations**. A tool set to **Ask** runs without asking you first.

In a task you start, Piper always asks before it reads a file that holds credentials or works in your own Chrome, in every approval mode. In work that runs without you, Piper refuses both. [Approvals and permissions](/control/approvals-and-permissions) covers each approval button.

## Hand Piper work that runs on its own

**Long-running tasks.** For a job too big for one conversation, select **Run until done** in the task's top bar. Piper then plans the job, splits it into steps, runs each step in its own conversation, and continues until the job is done. See [Long-running tasks](/autonomous/long-running-tasks).

**Scheduled tasks.** For work you want repeated, such as a weekly report, Piper runs the same task on a schedule you set. See [Scheduled tasks](/autonomous/autonomous-tasks).

Piper runs both without asking you, as it does with work it starts from your mail.

## Limitations

- **Piper works only while it's open.** Piper runs tasks only while it's open and your Mac is awake. A scheduled run or a mail check that comes due while Piper is closed runs once when you open Piper again.
- **Piper makes mistakes.** Read a result before you send it anywhere.
- **Piper starts work from Gmail and Google Calendar only.** Your other connected apps give Piper context for its tasks.
