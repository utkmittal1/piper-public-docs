Chief of Stuff is Piper working on its own, on a screen of its own in the sidebar. It checks the integrations you switch on as watched sources, does the work it judges worth doing, and puts each finished result on a card for you to review. Nothing it drafts goes out until you select **Send**.

A watched source is one of your integrations, switched on for Chief of Stuff to check. An integration is the connection Piper holds to an app you already use, such as Gmail, Slack, or Google Calendar. See [Integrations](/connections/integrations) for connecting one.

## The Chief of Stuff screen

Select **Chief of Stuff** in the sidebar. Three tabs divide it. **Ready** holds the cards waiting on you, **History** holds the record of every run, and **Settings** holds the watched sources, how eagerly Piper works, the notification hours, and your rules.

A run is one pass Chief of Stuff makes on its own. Piper opens a session, reads what changed in your watched sources, does the work, and declares what it produced. Each declared result becomes one card under **Ready**. You never schedule a run, and Chief of Stuff never appears in the **Autonomous tasks** list.

Work reaches Chief of Stuff two ways: from the sources you switch on, and from anything you hand it yourself.

<Note>
  One run takes on at most three pieces of work. Anything else Piper judged worth doing carries forward to the next run. The cap limits what one run does, not what it shows you: everything it finishes appears.
</Note>

## How Ready groups the cards

**Ready** groups cards by how soon they matter.

| Group | What sits there |
|---|---|
| **Before your next meeting** | Work tied to a meeting a few hours away or less |
| **Ready for you** | Everything else that is waiting |
| `3 more, mostly low-signal` | The rest, collapsed and closed until you open it |

The number in that last heading is how many cards sit inside it.

Each card carries the work itself, one line saying why Piper did it, and its buttons. There are four kinds.

- A message card carries the reply exactly as it will go out, with a line at the top naming who it would go to. Select **Send** to send that text, **Edit** to change it first, or **Copy** to put it on your clipboard. Editing turns the body into a box you type in, and **Send** becomes **Send edited**. The card reads **Waiting** until the message goes.
- A document card is a brief or a draft, already written into the `final-output/` folder of the session that produced it, and badged **Meeting prep** or **Draft**. Select **View** to open the whole thing. There is nothing to accept, because the file exists before the card appears.
- An answer card is the result itself, in full on the card, badged **Answer**. There is nothing to send or file.
- An offer is work Piper proposes rather than work it has done, badged **Not started**. Select **Go ahead** to start it.

**Refine** and **Dismiss** sit on every card. **Open session** sits beside them whenever Piper still has the session that did the work, which is the session **Refine** sends your feedback to.

<Note>
  A formatted email is the exception on a message card. **Send** becomes **Review**, **Edit** disappears, and selecting **Review** opens a **Review email** window with **To**, **Cc**, and **Subject** at the top and **Send** and **Cancel** in its footer. Only email and Slack messages can be sent from this screen at all. For anything else, use **Copy** and paste it where it belongs.
</Note>

Select **Refine** to give one line of feedback in answer to "What should Piper change?". On a message, document, or answer your line goes to the session that did the work, and that session opens so you can watch the rework. On an offer Piper rebuilds the proposal in place, without moving you anywhere.

**Dismiss** is permanent, and it means two different things. Dismissing a message or an offer teaches Piper that you did not want it, and later runs steer away from work like it. Dismissing a document or an answer clears the card from the list, and Piper reads nothing into it.

<Note>
  Every reaction teaches Chief of Stuff something. Sending a message, sending it after editing, copying it, asking for a rework, and dismissing a card each add one line to its lessons, which later runs read. Nothing else writes to them: Chief of Stuff records what you did, never its own opinion of the work.
</Note>

## Set up watching

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Chief of Stuff">
    Select **Chief of Stuff** in the sidebar, then the **Settings** tab.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Switch on a source">
    Under **Watched sources**, flip the switch beside an integration. Gmail, Slack, and Google Calendar are listed here from the start, whether or not you have connected them, and a row for an integration you have not connected says so and keeps its switch greyed out. Select **Add another source** to open **Watch another source**, then **Watch** on the integration you want.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wait for the source to be ready">
    A source you have just switched on reads **Piper is studying this source** for a while. Piper is building that source a fast check: a single read-only call it can replay later to tell whether anything has changed. It works one out in a session of its own and proves it against your live account. For an email or messaging source it also writes a voice guide, a note on how you write that it reads later when it drafts a reply. Wait for the row to settle at **Watching**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose how eagerly Piper works">
    Under **How eagerly Piper works**, pick **Conservative**, which asks before big work so you approve a proposal first, or **Proactive**, which does big work and tells you.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write your rules">
    Under **Your rules**, put standing instructions in your own words, one per line. Something like "Never draft replies to marketing email." Piper saves the text as you type, and these outrank anything it worked out for itself.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Watching is on exactly while at least one source is switched on. There is no master switch. Turn every source off and the watching stops, though Chief of Stuff still works on anything you hand it yourself.

<Note>
  Some integrations offer no call Piper can build a fast check out of. A source in that position settles at **Checked hourly. This source has no fast check.** It still works. Piper looks at it on the hour rather than every fifteen minutes.
</Note>

## Hand off work

You do not need a watched source to use Chief of Stuff. A paper-plane button sits at the bottom right of every screen, and `⌘⇧H` opens the same menu from anywhere. It offers **Quick question** and **Hand off work**. Select **Hand off work** to open the drawer titled **Hand off to Chief of Stuff**, type or dictate whatever is on your plate, and select **Hand off**. Piper works out the ask, does the work, and puts the result under **Ready**.

Each handed-off entry keeps its own status: `waiting`, then `working`, then `done`. A finished entry offers **Open session** for the session that did the work, and clears itself from the drawer a day later. Until then, select the pencil icon, labelled **Reword this ask**, or the cross beside it, labelled **Remove this ask**. Rewording keeps the same ask and sends Piper back to it. Removing stops unfinished work on it and leaves anything already delivered alone.

An ask you hand off outranks everything Chief of Stuff found in your sources, and it is the one thing a run never stays quiet about. A run that turned up nothing in your sources shows you nothing at all, but a finished ask always leaves a card: a draft, a file, a proposal, or an answer.

## How eagerly Piper works

Small, clear work is always finished either way. The setting decides what happens with big work, such as a full first draft of a document or deep prep for a major meeting.

| Setting | What Piper does with big work |
|---|---|
| **Conservative** | Asks first. You get an offer card, and Piper builds the work once you select **Go ahead**. |
| **Proactive** | Does the work and tells you, so you walk in prepared. |

Work where the approach is genuinely unclear comes back as an offer whatever the setting, because Piper does not guess at a call only you can make.

## The three checks and when they run

Chief of Stuff runs on three checks, each on its own clock. **History** names them: on each run's title, and on the wake-check rows, where they read hyphenated as `source-scan`, `time-lookahead`, and `daily-sweep`.

| Check | How often | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Source scan** | Every 15 minutes | Replays each watched source's fast check, and opens a run only where something has changed |
| **Time look-ahead** | Every hour | Reads the week ahead in a watched calendar, and covers any source with no fast check or still being set up |
| **Daily sweep** | Once a day at 8:00 in your local time | Picks up slower-burning work and anything the quick scans deferred |

Only the source scan can decide to do nothing. It replays each fast check and stays asleep unless the answer changed, so a quarter hour with nothing new opens no session at all. The other two do not work that way. The look-ahead and the sweep open a session whenever anything is in their scope, which means a watched calendar gets an hourly run whether or not it has moved.

Select **Check now** at the top of the page to run a source scan immediately, skipping both the clock and the fast checks. It reads **Checking…** while it runs.

## The History tab

**History** lists the runs Chief of Stuff has made, each named by what it actually did, with its summary, its results, and **Open transcript** to read the session behind it. It opens on **Runs that did work**, so a quiet check that found nothing stays hidden. Switch to **All runs** to see every one, search the history by what happened, and select **Show more** for older entries.

At the bottom sits a collapsed **Wake checks** group, carrying its own count, which records why Chief of Stuff ran or stayed asleep. Each row reads `woke` or `skipped`, then names the check and the reason.

## When Piper pings you

Under **Notification hours** you set the window in which Piper may ping you, with **From** and **Until**. It runs from 8:00 AM to 10:00 PM until you change it. Piper keeps working outside that window and holds the ping until the window opens. Work tied to a meeting a few hours away still pings.

A ping arrives titled **Ready for you**, carrying the reason line off the newest waiting card, and opens the Chief of Stuff screen. Turn it off under **Settings** > **Notifications**, where it and **Draft awaiting your approval** are listed separately. See [Notifications](/working/notifications).

<Note>
  Chief of Stuff works while Piper is open and your Mac is on and awake. A machine that is off, closed, or asleep runs no checks.
</Note>

<Warning>
  Piper watches only the sources you switch on, and only integrations you have already connected under **Settings** > **Integrations**. Chief of Stuff sends nothing itself: Piper refuses every write tool in these runs, in code rather than by instruction, so a message it writes cannot go anywhere until you select **Send**. If you ask it for a recurring result delivered to your inbox, that delivery runs as a [scheduled task](/autonomous/autonomous-tasks) on the schedule you agreed, not as a card.
</Warning>

<Note>
  Chief of Stuff also gets no memory. Piper leaves the facts it keeps about you out of these runs, so anything it should always do belongs under **Your rules**.
</Note>

<Note>
  Chief of Stuff pauses whenever Piper is signed out of your default AI provider. The screen reads "Chief of Stuff is paused. Piper is signed out.", **Check now** is greyed out, and no check runs. A banner at the top of the window carries **Sign in**. Piper picks the watching back up by itself within about five minutes of your signing in again. See [AI providers](/concepts/ai-providers) for the sign-in itself.
</Note>

## Start over

Under **Settings**, in **Start over**, select **Forget everything** and confirm with **Forget**. Piper deletes the watcher and everything Chief of Stuff learned: its checks, its voice guides, its lessons, and every card. Your **How eagerly Piper works** and **Notification hours** choices survive. Watching stays off until you switch a source back on.
