Chief of Stuff is Piper working without being asked. Everywhere else in Piper you open a session, explain what you need, and Piper does it. Chief of Stuff decides for itself. It watches the places your work actually arrives, your Gmail, your Slack, your Google Calendar, your Google Drive, works out what is worth doing, does it, and hands you each finished result on a card. You choose what it watches, and you approve what it sends.

Those places are called watched sources. A watched source is an app you have switched on for Chief of Stuff to check, and any app Piper connects to can be one. Piper has to be connected to the app first. See [Integrations](/connections/integrations).

## Why Chief of Stuff is worth turning on

The reason work sits on your list is rarely that you have nobody to do it. Asking is itself work. You have to open a session, give the context, and say what you want, so the reply you owe Priya waits, and the brief for tomorrow's meeting waits, because starting them costs the time you did not have.

Chief of Stuff takes the asking out. It flags the reply you still owe, preps the meeting before it lands, and starts the document somebody asked you for. It reads the week ahead in your calendar, so a Thursday deadline arrives with the work already prepared.

It is picky about what it takes on, which is what keeps it useful. Piper does the work that needs research, gathering material, or focused writing. It leaves alone anything you would answer in seconds, because a draft there is only noise.

So what you come back to is the work itself. A reply written with the numbers already pulled from the thread it answers. A one-page brief for your next meeting, naming who is coming and what each of them last asked you for. A first draft of the document somebody asked you to produce, with the gaps marked inline where only you can fill them.

None of it can leave on its own. Piper refuses every write tool inside a Chief of Stuff run, in code rather than by instruction, so a message it drafts sits on its card until you read it and select **Send**.

That work happens while you are away from it, with one honest limit. Chief of Stuff runs while Piper is open and your Mac is on and awake. A machine that is off, closed, or asleep runs no checks.

## Set up watching

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Chief of Stuff">
    Select **Chief of Stuff** in the sidebar, then the **Settings** tab.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Switch on a source">
    Under **Watched sources**, flip the switch beside the app you want watched. Gmail, Slack, and Google Calendar are listed here from the start, whether or not you have set them up, and a row for an app you have not set up says so and keeps its switch greyed out. To watch anything else you have connected, such as Google Drive or Notion, select **Add another source** to open **Watch another source**, then **Watch** on the one you want.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wait for the source to be ready">
    A source you have just switched on reads **Piper is studying this source** for a while. Piper is building that source a fast check: a single read-only call it can replay later to tell whether anything has changed. It works one out in a session of its own and proves it against your live account. For an email or messaging source it also writes a voice guide, a note on how you write that it reads later when it drafts a reply. Wait for the row to settle at **Watching**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose how eagerly Piper works">
    Under **How eagerly Piper works**, pick **Conservative**, which asks before big work so you approve a proposal first, or **Proactive**, which does big work and tells you.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write your rules">
    Under **Your rules**, put standing instructions in your own words, one per line. Something like "Never draft replies to marketing email." Piper saves the text as you type, and these outrank anything it worked out for itself.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Watching is on exactly while at least one source is switched on. There is no master switch. Turn every source off and the watching stops, though Chief of Stuff still works on anything you hand it yourself.

<Note>
  Some apps offer no call Piper can build a fast check out of. A source in that position settles at **Checked hourly. This source has no fast check.** It still works. Piper looks at it on the hour rather than every fifteen minutes.
</Note>

## The Chief of Stuff screen

Select **Chief of Stuff** in the sidebar. Three tabs divide it. **Ready** holds the work waiting on you, **History** holds the record of every run, and **Settings** holds the watched sources, how eagerly Piper works, the notification hours, and your rules.

A run is one pass Chief of Stuff makes on its own: Piper opens a session, reads what changed in your watched sources, and does the work. You never schedule a run, and Chief of Stuff never appears in the **Autonomous tasks** list.

Work reaches Chief of Stuff two ways: from the sources you switch on, and from anything you hand it yourself.

## What Chief of Stuff brings back

Everything Chief of Stuff finishes waits for you under **Ready**. What it hands over depends on the work.

- A reply you owe, drafted as an email or a Slack message and ready to send. Piper writes it in full, names who it goes to, and leaves a bracketed gap where a fact is one only you know.
- Prep for a meeting on your calendar. A page at most: who is coming, what the record already settled, what you will probably be asked to decide, and what to have open.
- A document, such as a first draft of something you were asked to produce or the research you needed for it. Piper marks each blocked decision inline as `[Open: ...]`, so you resolve them while you edit.
- An answer to a question you handed off, where the answer itself is the whole result and there is no file to keep.
- A proposal for work Piper has not started. It leads with the one angle Piper recommends and says what approving it buys you: a reply, a brief, a document.

A meeting brief and a document exist as files before you ever see them, in the `final-output/` folder of the session that produced them, so there is nothing to accept on either one.

<Note>
  Only email and Slack messages can be sent from Chief of Stuff. Piper drafts a reply for anything else too, but you have to **Copy** it and paste it where it belongs. A formatted email opens in a **Review email** window first, so you can check **To**, **Cc**, and **Subject** before it goes.
</Note>

Select **Refine** to give one line of feedback in answer to "What should Piper change?". On a reply, a document, or an answer your line goes to the session that did the work, and that session opens so you can watch the rework. On a proposal Piper rebuilds it in place, without moving you anywhere.

**Dismiss** is permanent, and it means two different things. Dismissing a reply or a proposal teaches Piper that you did not want it, and later runs steer away from work like it. Dismissing a document or an answer clears it from the list, and Piper reads nothing into it.

<Note>
  Every reaction teaches Chief of Stuff something. What you send, edit, copy, ask to be reworked, and dismiss all steer later runs, and Chief of Stuff records only what you did, never its own opinion of the work.
</Note>

## Hand off work

You do not need a watched source to use Chief of Stuff. Select **Hand off work**, or press `⌘⇧H` from anywhere in Piper, and type or dictate whatever is on your plate. Piper works out the ask, does the work, and puts the result under **Ready**.

Reach for this when something is on your mind and you have no time to work out how to ask for it. One line is enough, and Piper picks it up within fifteen minutes.

An ask you hand off clears itself a day after Piper finishes it. Until then you can reword it, which keeps the same ask and sends Piper back to it, or remove it, which stops unfinished work and leaves anything already delivered alone.

Anything you hand off comes first, ahead of whatever Chief of Stuff found on its own. It is also the one thing a run never stays quiet about: a run that turned up nothing in your sources shows you nothing at all, while a finished ask always comes back with something.

## The three checks and when they run

Chief of Stuff runs on three checks, each on its own clock.

| Check | How often | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Source scan** | Every 15 minutes | Looks for what has changed in your watched sources |
| **Time look-ahead** | Every hour | Reads the week ahead in a watched calendar, so work that needs lead time starts early |
| **Daily sweep** | Once a day at 8:00 in your local time | Picks up slower-burning work |

Select **Check now** to run a source scan immediately, without waiting for the clock.

## When Piper pings you

Piper pings you when something it finished is waiting for you. Work tied to a meeting a few hours away reaches you whatever the hour.

Set the hours you are willing to be pinged under **Notification hours**, which runs from 8:00 AM to 10:00 PM until you change it. Piper keeps working outside that window either way, and holds the ping until the window opens. To stop the pings altogether, use **Settings** > **Notifications**. See [Notifications](/working/notifications).

## Start over

Under **Settings**, in **Start over**, select **Forget everything**. Piper deletes the watcher and everything Chief of Stuff learned: its checks, its voice guides, its lessons, and every result waiting on you. Your **How eagerly Piper works** and **Notification hours** choices survive. Watching stays off until you switch a source back on.
