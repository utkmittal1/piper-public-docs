A long-running task is a job too big to finish in one sitting, such as researching a hundred accounts, auditing every page of a site, or working through a folder of contracts. Piper writes a plan, breaks the job into steps, runs them in rounds, and keeps going until the job is done.

## Start a long-running task

<Steps>
  <Step title="Do the thinking in an ordinary task">
    Start a normal task and work out what you want. Gather the sources, settle the rules, and make any files the job will need. Piper carries everything you agree here into the long-running task.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select Run until done">
    Select **Run until done** in the top bar. You can select it once Piper finishes its reply. Piper switches the conversation to **Autopilot** at the same time, because a long-running task works with nobody watching it. If the conversation has folders you added, Piper first asks you to confirm that the task can change files in them without asking.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Agree what done looks like">
    This conversation becomes the **Orchestrator**, and it keeps every message and every file already in it. Piper reads back through the conversation, asks about anything still unclear, and tells you what it's about to do. Answer Piper's questions, then tell Piper to go ahead.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Watch it work">
    Piper writes the plan and starts the first steps. You stay in the same conversation. The conversation's row stays in your **Tasks** list, now as a long-running task. A task you started inside a project belongs to that project and appears in the project's list too.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  You decide whether the work is big enough for a long-running task. Piper can also start one when you ask for it in a conversation. Piper then writes a handoff brief, opens a new long-running task with its own **Orchestrator**, and moves the files the work needs into the task's folder. Your conversation stays an ordinary conversation, and Piper adds a card to it that reads **Runs until it is done**.
</Note>

<Note>
  You can select **Run until done** only in an ordinary task. A conversation inside a long-running or scheduled task can't become a task of its own, because a task can't hold another task. A question you ask in [Ask Piper](/working/describing-what-you-need#ask-piper-a-quick-question) can't become a long-running task either.
</Note>

## The Orchestrator and the steps it hands out

An ordinary task is one conversation, described on [Tasks and the workspace](/concepts/sessions-and-workspace). A long-running task holds many conversations. The conversation you started the work in becomes the conversation that plans the job, and Piper gives every step a conversation of its own.

Piper calls that planning conversation the **Orchestrator**. In the Orchestrator, Piper decides what finished looks like, writes the plan down, hands each piece of work out as a step, checks each step's result against that definition, and says when the job is done.

A step is a real piece of work one conversation can finish on its own, start to finish, whatever the other steps in its round do. Piper writes every step's result into the task's own folder, which outlives every conversation in the task.

Piper keeps the plan in a file called `plan.md`, in the Orchestrator's workspace folder. Piper writes the plan before handing anything out and updates it every time work comes back, so the plan survives a conversation that gets cleared or restarted. To read it, select **Update task** on the task's page to open the Orchestrator.

### Rounds of steps

Piper hands work out in rounds. A round holds a limited number of steps, and every step in a round runs at the same time. Piper starts the next round only after every step in the round settles.

When the last step of a round settles, Piper posts in the Orchestrator how each step went: what the step was asked to do, what it reported, and one word for how it ended. That word is `delivered`, `failed`, `stopped, needs something`, `interrupted`, or `unreported`. Piper then updates the plan in the Orchestrator and either opens the next round or says the job is finished. Piper repeats that cycle until the job is done.

Piper can carry on a step that failed, reported `Needs you`, was cut off, or ended unreported, instead of replacing it. Piper picks the step back up in its own conversation and its own folder, with everything the step already did. For a step that finished, or a step whose workspace Piper has already cleared away, Piper writes a fresh assignment instead.

<Note>
  In the **Orchestrator**'s conversation, Piper labels each round's card as a batch, like `Batch 3`, with a badge showing how many steps the round holds. Piper caps the size of a round for two reasons. Piper runs a limited number of conversations at once. Steps that run together also can't see what the others decided, so a wider round leaves more steps working without each other's decisions.
</Note>

### Steps that end without reporting

A step that ends without saying how it went settles as `unreported`. Its card shows an **Unreported** badge and the line "This step ended without saying how it went." The step's reply and its folder are still there, so Piper settles the step later, in the Orchestrator.

In the Orchestrator, Piper reads what the step produced and marks the step as delivered or failed, with one line on what the step produced. That line replaces the report on the step's card. Until Piper marks the step, the card keeps the **Unreported** badge. Piper can also carry the step on instead.

## What a step is given

Piper gives every step three things beyond what an ordinary task gets.

The first is its own assignment, which Piper writes in the Orchestrator and hands over as the first message in that step. Open the step to read it. The second is the task's **Instructions**, the rules every step follows, which you can read and edit yourself in the **Instructions** box on the task page. The third is a short snapshot of each of the most recent steps, with the path to that step's full work log, so a step can pick up what came before it. For a task inside a project, Piper also gives each step the project's instructions, sources, and memory, and tells it what the project's own conversations have been doing.

Piper keeps the Orchestrator's conversation, and every conversation you opened on the task, out of each step. Piper also withholds the tools for creating tasks from every step. In the Orchestrator, Piper aims to keep the plan out of the instructions, so no step starts doing work that belongs to another step. Piper doesn't enforce that aim, so a plan written into the instructions reaches every step.

Steps in the same round start together, so each step works without seeing what the other steps are doing. That's why every assignment has to say where the step's result goes.

<Note>
  A step that's already running keeps the instructions it started with. Only steps created after a change, whether you made it or Piper made it in the Orchestrator, follow the new instructions.
</Note>

<Note>
  Piper starts every step on **Autopilot** because nobody is watching, so Piper acts without stopping to ask. When a step can't get past a problem, Piper reports `Needs you` on the step and names what it needs, instead of guessing. A step can't email or invite anyone. When the work needs that, the step says so in its report. Piper also withholds the Chrome you're signed in to from every step, and refuses a credential file outright, because nobody is there to approve either. [Approvals and permissions](/control/approvals-and-permissions) covers both rules.
</Note>

## Follow the work and pause it

The task's page shows the whole job. To read Piper's reasoning, select **Update task** to open the Orchestrator.

The **Steps** tab shows its count, like `Steps · 12`, and lists every step on its own card. A card shows one of these labels when the step needs your attention:

- `Needs you` when the step stopped to ask you for something
- `Failed` when the step couldn't finish
- `Trying again due now` when Piper is about to restart the step, or `Retry held` while the task is paused
- `Unreported` when the step ended without saying how it went, until Piper settles it in the Orchestrator

On a step's second or third start, the card shows the attempt, like `Attempt 2 of 3`, while the step runs. Select a card to see what the step reported and the files it produced. To read everything the step did, select **Open step** on its card.

Select **Pause** in the header to stop the task. Piper stops each step running at that moment and marks it "Stopped when the task was paused." Select **Resume** to start the task again. Piper picks those steps back up before it plans anything new in the Orchestrator.

<Tip>
  The **Instructions** box on the task's page holds the rules every step follows. Select **Artifacts** in the top bar to see the task's own folder, with everything the job delivers and anything Piper keeps from one step to the next. For a task outside a project, Piper keeps the folder under `PiperData/projects` in your home folder. For a task inside a project, Piper keeps the folder in the project's Piper folder, under `tasks/`. Select **Memory** in the top bar for the facts Piper keeps for this task alone. See [Memory](/context/memory).
</Tip>

## Talk to a task while it runs

The **Conversations** tab lists the conversations you have opened on this task, with the count beside its name. Select **Start a conversation**, type your first message, and Piper opens the conversation inside the task and takes you into it.

Open a conversation of your own when you want to change the task without interrupting the work in the Orchestrator. From your own conversation, Piper can change the task's name, description, and instructions, rework the plan, and set the next round of steps going.

The **Orchestrator** is where Piper reports back. When a round of steps finishes, Piper writes the results there and plans the next round from the same place.

You can steer one task from several conversations at once. Piper gives each conversation the current plan and instructions, so a change you make in one conversation shows up in the others.

<Note>
  After you send a message to the **Orchestrator**, Piper waits before it asks there for new steps, so you have time to answer a question Piper asked you before it opens more steps.
</Note>

## Ask a finished task for more

Use **Resume** on the task page for a task you paused yourself. If you ask a paused task for more work, Piper tells you the task is paused and asks you to resume it there first.

## A task inside a project

Start a long-running task from a conversation in a project and the task belongs to that project. Its row appears in the project's list. To get back to the project from the task's page, select **Back**, or open **Projects** in the sidebar.

A task inside a project follows the project's instructions and can use its shared files, its memory, and its sources, on top of its own. Piper keeps the task's folder inside the project and delivers the task's results there by default.

The task can write only to the project's Piper folder. For a project Piper manages, that's the whole project folder. For a project you pointed at a folder of your own, it's the subfolder Piper created there, so your own files around it stay out of reach. The task can also write in the folders it took from the conversation when you selected **Run until done**.

Only a long-running task can sit inside a project. Piper always keeps a scheduled task outside any project.

<Warning>
  When you delete a project, Piper stops and deletes every long-running task in it, with its steps and its folder.
</Warning>

## How a job finishes

Piper refuses to call a job finished in three cases: a step is still running, the task has never opened a step, or a step is still unreported. Piper says so in the **Orchestrator**'s conversation and keeps working on what would actually finish the job. Past those checks, Piper decides in the Orchestrator when the work is done, with no further check. Read what the task produced before you rely on it.

## When a job gives up

A job stops before it's finished in two ways. In the Orchestrator, Piper can say outright that the job can't go on and name what it needs from you. Piper then pauses the task.

Or Piper can stop opening steps in the Orchestrator. When nothing is running, Piper asks in the Orchestrator what comes next, and pauses the task after several passes in a row that open no step and declare nothing. Piper then tells you "It stopped saying what to do next, so nothing more is running. Resume it to carry on." Asking the task for more work also starts it again.

Piper notifies you when the whole job delivers and when it stops itself. Both notifications open with the task's own name, then "is done" or "stopped itself". Turn either off under **Settings**, then **Notifications**, where they are listed as **Job finished** and **Task stopped itself**. See [Notifications](/working/notifications).

<Warning>
  Piper moves a long-running task forward only while Piper is open and your Mac is on and awake. Quitting Piper doesn't pause the task. Piper marks any step that was running as cut off, and picks it back up in the folder it was using the next time you open Piper.
</Warning>

<Note>
  Every restart of a step counts as one of its three attempts, whether Piper closed under it or you paused the task. Piper gives up on a step cut off three times. Select **Pause** before a long shutdown, and **Resume** when you're back.
</Note>
