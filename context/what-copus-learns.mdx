Copus builds a People list, a guide to how you write, and a set of facts about your work. Copus learns from your mail when you connect Gmail, from your conversations with Copus, and from what you do with the work Copus starts for you. You review and edit what Copus keeps on the **About you** page in the sidebar.

This page covers how Copus learns about you and how you review what Copus keeps. For how Copus uses your memory, see [Memory](/context/memory).

## What Copus learns

Each kind of knowledge has its own tab on the **About you** page.

| Tab | What Copus keeps |
|---|---|
| **People** | Each person you exchange mail with, ranked by how much and how recently you write to them. For the people at the top of the list, Copus writes notes on who they are, what you discuss, and how each of you writes to the other. |
| **How you write** | A guide to your writing: how formal you are with different people, how you open and sign off, how long and how structured your messages are, and how you use emoji, exclamation marks, and formatting. Each point quotes mail you sent. |
| **Memory** | Short facts about your work and your life: what you do and where, your recurring projects and commitments, the people and groups around you, and the work you want Copus to take on or leave alone. |

## When Copus learns

| When | What Copus does |
|---|---|
| When you connect Gmail | Reads your recent sent mail and builds your People list. Then writes your writing guide, saves facts about you to memory, and writes notes on the people at the top of your People list. |
| Every 15 minutes | Adds the people in your new mail to your People list |
| During a conversation | Saves a fact to memory while it replies, when you tell Copus something worth keeping. A note in the conversation shows each save, with **Undo** and **Edit**. See [Memory](/context/memory). |
| Weekly | Reviews that week's results and updates your writing guide, the notes on the people in that week's tasks, and your memory |

In the weekly review, Copus reads which results you send unchanged, edit before sending, refine, dismiss, or decline. From that record, Copus saves facts under **Proactive help** about the work you want Copus to take on and the work you want it to leave alone. Copus uses these facts only when it decides what work to start on its own. Copus saves a fact only when it sees a pattern across several tasks.

## Review and edit your People list

Open **About you** in the sidebar and select **People**. To find a person, type a name or email address into **Search people…**.

Select a person to open their details. You can edit two sections:

- **Who they are**: Copus's note on who the person is
- **Notes**: what you and the person discuss, and how each of you writes to the other

To change either section, select the pencil icon (**Edit**), change the text, and select **Save**. Copus may revise your text in the weekly review. To see a section's earlier text, select the clock icon (**Earlier versions**). Each earlier version names who replaced the text: you or Copus.

When Copus matches two entries to the same person, Copus merges them into one entry.

## Review and edit your writing guide

Open **About you** in the sidebar and select **How you write**. Copus reads this guide before it drafts a reply or a message in your name.

To change a section, select the pencil icon (**Edit this section**), change the text, and select **Save**. To remove a section, delete its text and select **Save**. Copus may revise the guide in the weekly review.

## See the facts Copus saves about you

Copus saves what it learns about you as facts in your memory. These facts come from your sent mail when you connect Gmail, from your conversations, and from the weekly review. To see these facts, open **About you** in the sidebar and select **Memory**.

[Memory](/context/memory) covers how Copus uses your memory, and how to add, edit, and remove facts.

## Stop Copus from learning from your mail

To stop Copus from reading your mail, open **Settings** > **Integrations**, select Gmail, and disconnect it. Copus keeps your People list, writing guide, and memory, and stops updating your People list and writing guide from Gmail. Copus also loses access to Gmail in tasks you start.

Copus keeps saving facts from your conversations after you disconnect Gmail. To stop Copus from keeping a particular fact, remove the fact.

## Limitations

- **Copus learns how you write from Gmail only.** The **How you write** tab has a Slack option, and Copus leaves the Slack guide empty.
- **You can edit only the notes on a person.** You can't delete a person, rename a person, change a person's rank, or separate two people Copus merged.
- **The How you write tab shows only the current guide.** Copus keeps earlier versions of your writing guide on your Mac.
- **Copus sends what it learns to your AI provider when it uses it.** Copus stores your People list, writing guide, and memory on your Mac. Copus sends your memory with every task, a person's details with the threads that person is in, and your writing guide when Copus drafts in your name. See [Your data stays local](/control/your-data-stays-local).
