Piper builds a People list, a guide to how you write, and a set of facts about your work. Piper learns from your mail when you connect Gmail, from your conversations with Piper, and from what you do with the work Piper starts for you. You review and edit what Piper keeps on the **About you** page in the sidebar.

This page covers how Piper learns about you and how you review what Piper keeps. For how Piper uses your memory, see [Memory](/context/memory).

## What Piper learns

Each kind of knowledge has its own tab on the **About you** page.

| Tab | What Piper keeps |
|---|---|
| **People** | Each person you exchange mail with, ranked by how much and how recently you write to them. For the people at the top of the list, Piper writes notes on who they are, what you discuss, and how each of you writes to the other. |
| **How you write** | A guide to your writing: how formal you are with different people, how you open and sign off, how long and how structured your messages are, and how you use emoji, exclamation marks, and formatting. Each point quotes mail you sent. |
| **Memory** | Short facts about your work and your life: what you do and where, your recurring projects and commitments, the people and groups around you, and the work you want Piper to take on or leave alone. |

## When Piper learns

| When | What Piper does |
|---|---|
| When you connect Gmail | Reads your recent sent mail and builds your People list. Then writes your writing guide, saves facts about you to memory, and writes notes on the people at the top of your People list. |
| Every 15 minutes | Adds the people in your new mail to your People list |
| During a conversation | Saves a fact to memory while it replies, when you tell Piper something worth keeping. A note in the conversation shows each save, with **Undo** and **Edit**. See [Memory](/context/memory). |
| Weekly | Reviews that week's results and updates your writing guide, the notes on the people in that week's tasks, and your memory |

In the weekly review, Piper reads which results you send unchanged, edit before sending, refine, dismiss, or decline. From that record, Piper saves facts under **Proactive help** about the work you want Piper to take on and the work you want it to leave alone. Piper uses these facts only when it decides what work to start on its own. Piper saves a fact only when it sees a pattern across several tasks.

## Review and edit your People list

Open **About you** in the sidebar and select **People**. To find a person, type a name or email address into **Search people…**.

Select a person to open their details. You can edit two sections:

- **Who they are**: Piper's note on who the person is
- **Notes**: what you and the person discuss, and how each of you writes to the other

To change either section, select the pencil icon (**Edit**), change the text, and select **Save**. Piper may revise your text in the weekly review. To see a section's earlier text, select the clock icon (**Earlier versions**). Each earlier version names who replaced the text: you or Piper.

When Piper matches two entries to the same person, Piper merges them into one entry.

## Review and edit your writing guide

Open **About you** in the sidebar and select **How you write**. Piper reads this guide before it drafts a reply or a message in your name.

To change a section, select the pencil icon (**Edit this section**), change the text, and select **Save**. To remove a section, delete its text and select **Save**. Piper may revise the guide in the weekly review.

## See the facts Piper saves about you

Piper saves what it learns about you as facts in your memory. These facts come from your sent mail when you connect Gmail, from your conversations, and from the weekly review. To see these facts, open **About you** in the sidebar and select **Memory**.

[Memory](/context/memory) covers how Piper uses your memory, and how to add, edit, and remove facts.

## Stop Piper from learning from your mail

To stop Piper from reading your mail, open **Settings** > **Integrations**, select Gmail, and disconnect it. Piper keeps your People list, writing guide, and memory, and stops updating your People list and writing guide from Gmail. Piper also loses access to Gmail in tasks you start.

Piper keeps saving facts from your conversations after you disconnect Gmail. To stop Piper from keeping a particular fact, remove the fact.

## Limitations

- **Piper learns how you write from Gmail only.** The **How you write** tab has a Slack option, and Piper leaves the Slack guide empty.
- **You can edit only the notes on a person.** You can't delete a person, rename a person, change a person's rank, or separate two people Piper merged.
- **The How you write tab shows only the current guide.** Piper keeps earlier versions of your writing guide on your Mac.
- **Piper sends what it learns to your AI provider when it uses it.** Piper stores your People list, writing guide, and memory on your Mac. Piper sends your memory with every task, a person's details with the threads that person is in, and your writing guide when Piper drafts in your name. See [Your data stays local](/control/your-data-stays-local).
