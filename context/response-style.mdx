title: "Response style"
description: "Choose how long Copus's replies run, what tone they take, how much structure they carry, and whether they use emoji. Set the four controls in Settings and every task follows them."
---

Response style is four controls for how Copus writes back to you: **Response Length**, **Tone**, **Formatting**, and **Emoji**. Each control has two or three options. Copus reads the options you picked when a task opens, and again on your next message after you change a control.

The controls apply in full to the replies Copus writes to you in conversation. They apply only in part to a written deliverable, such as a document, an email, or a post you asked Copus to produce. See [What the controls don't change](#what-the-controls-dont-change).

## Change how Copus writes back

Select **Instructions** in the sidebar, then select an option under **Response Length**, **Tone**, **Formatting**, or **Emoji**.

Copus saves your choice the moment you select an option, and shows **Saving…** and then **Saved ✓** beside the control you changed.

| Control | Options | Starts on |
|---|---|---|
| **Response Length** | Concise, Balanced, Detailed | Balanced |
| **Tone** | Formal, Neutral, Casual | Neutral |
| **Formatting** | Structured, Balanced, Prose | Balanced |
| **Emoji** | None, Occasional | Occasional |

Copus applies a change to a task that's already open on your next message in that task. Copus finishes a reply already under way in the style it started in.

## What each option changes

### Response Length

- **Concise.** Copus leads with the answer or the action taken, and cuts preamble and filler. Where one sentence covers the answer, Copus writes one sentence.
- **Balanced.** Copus matches the length to the question. Copus answers a simple question in a few sentences and a complex question in a few paragraphs. Copus includes reasoning where it helps you follow the answer, and skips it where the point is obvious.
- **Detailed.** Copus gives context and background before the specifics, walks through a process step by step, and answers the obvious follow-up questions in the same reply. Where Copus is unsure whether a useful detail belongs, Copus includes it.

### Tone

- **Formal.** Copus writes complete sentences with exact vocabulary, and uses no contractions or slang. The replies read like business correspondence.
- **Neutral.** Copus writes in a direct, professional voice, with contractions where they sound natural. Copus avoids jargon unless you use it first or the subject demands it.
- **Casual.** Copus writes in a relaxed, conversational voice. Copus uses light humor and informal phrasing, and avoids forced enthusiasm.

### Formatting

- **Structured.** Copus formats for scanning, with headings between topics, bullets for collections, numbered lists for ordered steps, tables for comparisons, and bold for key terms. You can get the gist from the headings and the bold text alone.
- **Balanced.** Copus picks the form to fit the content. Copus puts three items in a sentence, five options in a table, and a concept in a paragraph.
- **Prose.** Copus writes short, connected paragraphs. Copus keeps headings, bullets, and tables for ordered steps, explicit comparisons, code, and data that belongs in a table.

### Emoji

- **None.** Copus uses no emoji in its own replies, status updates, or summaries. Copus still uses emoji in something you asked it to write whose format usually carries them, such as a social post.
- **Occasional.** Copus uses emoji rarely and with a purpose, such as a status marker, a section cue, or warmth in a conversational reply. Copus keeps emoji out of formal or professional content, and never puts several emoji in one sentence.

## What the controls don't change

The four controls cover length, tone, structure, and emoji. Write anything else you want Copus to do the same way every time in your standing instructions, in the box under **Custom Instructions** on the same **Instructions** screen. See [Instructions and profile](/context/instructions-and-profile). When you repeat a preference Copus stopped following, Copus keeps the preference in memory as a rule and sends the rule with every message. See [Keep a preference as a rule](/context/memory#keep-a-preference-as-a-rule).

A written deliverable is something you asked Copus to produce as a piece of work, such as a document, an email, a post, or a report. The controls shape a written deliverable only in part. For each control, the table shows what Copus follows instead.

| Control | What Copus follows instead |
|---|---|
| **Response Length** | What you asked for, and what the task needs |
| **Tone** | What you asked for |
| **Formatting** | The conventions of that format |
| **Emoji** | Under **None**, the format's own conventions. Under **Occasional**, Copus still keeps emoji out of formal or professional content |

Under **Concise**, Copus still writes a full report when you ask for a report. Under **Casual**, Copus still writes an email in the voice you asked for.

You set one style for every task. To change the style for one piece of work, write the style into that project's or that task's instructions.

When you ask Copus to have its work reviewed, Copus starts a separate review session titled **Review:** followed by the file's name. Copus runs the review session without your response style, with each control on its starting option: **Balanced**, **Neutral**, **Balanced**, and **Occasional**.

<Note>
  Copus sends your response style to your [AI provider](/concepts/ai-providers) alongside your instructions and your memory. After you change a control, Copus sends the change with your next message. See [Your data stays local](/control/your-data-stays-local).
</Note>
