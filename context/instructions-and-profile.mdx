Standing instructions and your profile are two things you write once and Piper carries into every task. Instructions say how you want work done. The profile says who Piper is working for: your name, your email address, and a short note about you.

## Standing instructions

Standing instructions are rules Piper follows in every task.

In **Settings**, select **Instructions**, then type your rules into **Custom Instructions** in plain sentences. Piper saves as you type and shows when it last saved. There is no save button.

Write the things you would otherwise say twice a week:

- "Always end with the next step."
- "I work in US spelling."
- "Never send anything outbound without showing me the draft first."
- "When you cite a number, say where it came from."

An edit reaches tasks that are already open. Piper picks it up on your next message in that task.

## Your profile

Your profile tells Piper who it is working for. Piper reads it at the start of every task, alongside your instructions.

Open **Settings**, select **Profile**, then fill in the fields under **About you**. There is a second way in: select your name at the bottom of the sidebar, then the row with your name at the top of the menu. It reads **Set up profile** until you have entered one.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Enter your name">
    Type the name Piper should call you into **Name**. It holds up to 100 characters.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter your email">
    Type your address into **Email**. It has to look like a real address, and it holds up to 200 characters.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write a short about-me note">
    In **About me**, write a few sentences on your role and how you like to receive work: what you do, what you are working towards, and the shape you want answers in. It holds up to 2,000 characters.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Piper saves each field as you type, and again when you leave it. A field that is too long is refused with the reason rather than shortened to fit.

<Note>
  What you enter here also lands on the **Memory** page, under **Personal info**, as your name, your email, and your about-me note. Those rows carry no edit or remove controls there. Change the field on the **Profile** page and the memory record follows.
</Note>

## How instructions and the profile differ from memory

Instructions, the profile, and memory come from different places.

| | Where it comes from | Where you change it |
|---|---|---|
| **Standing instructions** | You type them | **Settings > Instructions** |
| **Profile** | You type it | **Settings > Profile** |
| **[Memory](/context/memory)** | Piper picks facts up as you work, and you can add your own | **Settings > Memory** |

Instructions tell Piper how to behave. The profile tells Piper who it is working for. Memory is the running record of facts Piper has picked up along the way.

All three reach every task. Inside a project, the project's own instructions come after these and take priority where the two disagree. Inside a long-running task that belongs to a project, the project's instructions come first and the task's own follow them.

<Warning>
  Piper never keeps a secret value in your profile or your memory. Text that looks like a credential, an API key, a token, or a private key is refused on save, in the profile fields and in memory alike. Put credentials in [Secrets](/connections/secrets), where they sit in your Mac's keychain.
</Warning>
