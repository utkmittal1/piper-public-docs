Standing instructions are directions you write in plain sentences for Copus to follow in every task. Your profile is three fields that say who Copus works for: your name, your email address, and a short note about you. You write both in **Settings**, and Copus reads both at the start of every task.

Instructions say how you want work done. The profile says who the work is for.

This page covers how to:

- [Tell instructions, the profile, and memory apart](#instructions-the-profile-and-memory)
- [Write your standing instructions](#write-your-standing-instructions)
- [Fill in your profile](#fill-in-your-profile), and find those fields again in memory

## Instructions, the profile, and memory

Copus reads your instructions, your profile, and your memory at the start of every task.

|  | What it holds | Who writes it |
|---|---|---|
| Standing instructions | Directions Copus follows in every task | You |
| **Profile** | Your name, your email address, and a short note about you | You. Copus fills in your name and email address when you sign in to Copus |
| **[Memory](/context/memory)** | Short facts about you and your work, and rules Copus follows on every message | Copus as you work, and you |

Write an instruction for a direction you want followed in every task, such as how every report should open. Leave facts about you to memory, where Copus saves facts as you work and you can add your own. When you repeat a preference Copus stopped following, Copus keeps that preference in memory as a rule. See [Keep a preference as a rule](/context/memory#keep-a-preference-as-a-rule).

The four controls for length, tone, formatting, and emoji sit on the same **Instructions** screen, above the instructions box. To set them, see [Response style](/context/response-style).

## Write your standing instructions

Select **Instructions** in the sidebar, then type your instructions into the box under **Custom Instructions**. Copus saves as you type, and again when you leave the box. You can write as much as you want.

Write directions you want Copus to follow in every task, such as:

- "Always end with the next step."
- "I work in US spelling."
- "Never send anything outbound without showing me the draft first."
- "When you cite a number, say where it came from."

Copus shows **Saving…** under the box while it saves and **Saved ✓** when it finishes.

Copus applies an edit to a task that's already open when you send your next message in that task. Copus finishes a reply already under way with the instructions it started with.

## Fill in your profile

Copus fills in your name and email address from your Google account when you sign in to Copus.

To edit your profile, use either of these:

- In **Settings**, select **Profile**. The three fields sit under the heading **About you**.
- Select your name at the bottom of the sidebar, then select your name again at the top of the menu that opens. Copus opens the same three fields in a dialog titled **Profile**. Before you enter a name, both places read **Set up profile**.

The **About you** heading in **Settings** > **Profile** holds only these three fields. The **About you** page in the sidebar is a separate page, with your People list, your writing guide, and your memory. See [What Copus learns about you](/context/what-copus-learns).

<Steps>
  <Step title="Enter your name">
    Type the name Copus should call you into **Name**. Copus capitalizes the first letter of each word.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter your email">
    Type your address into **Email**. Copus accepts any text shaped like an email address.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write a short about-me note">
    In **About me**, write a few sentences on your role and how you like to receive work: what you do, what you're working toward, and the form you want answers in.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Copus saves the profile as you type, and again when you leave a field. When a field runs too long, or the email address isn't shaped like an address, Copus refuses the whole save and shows a message that names the field and the problem.

### Find your profile again in memory

Copus writes the same three fields into your global memory, under **Personal info**, in the same save. Copus puts a short label in front of your name and your email address. Copus saves your about-me note as you wrote it, and trims blank space from the start and the end.

On the **Memory** page, each of the three facts shows **Profile** as its source and has no edit or remove controls. To change a profile fact, edit the field in **Settings** > **Profile**, and Copus rewrites the matching fact. When you clear a field, Copus deletes the matching fact.

## Which instructions win

Copus reads four sets of standing instructions and follows the narrowest set where two disagree.

| Set | Where you write it | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| Your standing instructions | **Instructions** in the sidebar | Every task, including the tasks Copus starts on its own |
| A project's instructions | The **Instructions** panel on that project's page | Every task in that project |
| A multi-step or scheduled task's instructions | The **Instructions** box on that task's page | Every run and every step of that task |
| One task's instructions | The **Instructions** panel beside the task itself | That task alone |

A project's instructions take priority over your standing instructions. The instructions you write beside one task take priority over all the others.

Copus saves each of the three panels the way it saves the **Settings** box: as you type, and again when you leave the box. See [Projects](/concepts/projects) for a project's panel and [Multi-step tasks](/autonomous/multi-step-tasks) for a task's.

<Note>
  When you ask Copus to have its work reviewed, Copus starts a separate review session titled **Review:** followed by the file's name. The review session runs without your standing instructions, your project's instructions, and your response style. Copus writes what you asked for, and the instructions set for the task or its project, into the review request instead.
</Note>

## What Copus refuses to store

<Warning>
  Copus refuses text that reads like a credential when it saves your profile or your memory. Copus refuses the whole field or fact, and keeps none of it. Put credentials in [Secrets](/connections/secrets), where Copus stores them in your Mac's keychain.
</Warning>

Copus refuses these kinds of text:

- A value written after a word such as password, token, secret, credential, or api key, followed by a colon or an equals sign.
- A key in a shape several services use, such as a key that begins `sk-`, `ghp_`, or `xoxb-`.
- A private key block.
- A long unbroken run of letters and digits, such as an encoded key or a hash.

Copus matches the shape of the text, so Copus misses some credentials. Copus still saves a credential written as ordinary prose. Copus also refuses an ordinary sentence that carries a long identifier, such as a file hash.

<Note>
  Copus sends your instructions, your profile, your memory, and your response style to your [AI provider](/concepts/ai-providers) when a task connects. After you change any of them, Copus sends the new set with your next message. Copus stores all of them on your Mac. To see what leaves your Mac, see [Your data stays local](/control/your-data-stays-local).
</Note>
