Copus keeps short facts about you between tasks, such as "Prefers concise answers" or "Anjali leads design." Copus also keeps rules, and follows each rule on every message. When you mention something worth keeping, Copus saves a fact while it replies. Copus also saves facts it learns from your mail and from what you do with the tasks it starts on its own. You can add, edit, and remove facts yourself.

## Memory, instructions, response style, and knowledge

Copus reads your memory alongside your instructions, response style, and knowledge sources.

| | What it holds | Who writes it |
|---|---|---|
| **Memory** | Short facts about you and your work, and rules Copus follows on every message | Copus as you work, and you |
| **[Instructions and profile](/context/instructions-and-profile)** | Standing instructions for every task, and your name, email, and about-me note | You |
| **[Response style](/context/response-style)** | The length, tone, formatting, and emoji of Copus's replies | You |
| **[Knowledge sources](/context/knowledge-sources)** | Folders of your own documents Copus searches | You |

Write an instruction for a standing direction you set up front, such as how every report should open. Every task reads your global rules. Copus creates a rule when you repeat a preference it stopped following. Use memory for facts Copus learns as you work.

## Choose the level a fact belongs at

A fact belongs at the narrowest level where it stays true.

| Level | What belongs there | What reads it |
|---|---|---|
| **Global** | What stays true about you everywhere: who you are, the people around you, what you prefer, how you like work done | Every task you start |
| **Project** | Decisions and constraints for one project | Every task in that project, including scheduled and multi-step tasks in the project |
| **Session** | What applies to one conversation only, such as "use US spelling, just for this" | That conversation only |

Copus keeps a scheduled task's own facts on the task's page. The task also reads the facts of its project.

Keep global memory for facts about you that stay true from month to month. A deadline stops being true within weeks, so save a deadline at project or session level, or leave it out of memory.

### Categories for global facts

Copus files each global fact under a category, and groups the facts under these headings on the **Memory** page.

| Category | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| **Personal info** | Who you are, where you are, your contact details |
| **Team** | Teammates, relationships, who does what around you |
| **Preferences** | What you like: formats, tools, how answers should read |
| **Work style** | How you plan, review, and want work run |
| **Proactive help** | What Copus should take on, suggest, or leave alone when it starts work on its own |

Copus reads facts under **Proactive help** only when it decides what work to start from your apps. Copus saves most of these facts in its weekly review. See [What Copus learns about you](/context/what-copus-learns).

Project and session facts have the type **Fact** or **Rule** instead of a category.

## Keep a preference as a rule

Copus follows a rule on every message, such as "Put a one-line summary at the top of every report." Copus reads facts when a conversation starts, and sends every rule again with each message you send, so Copus has every rule through a long conversation.

The first time you state a preference, Copus saves it as a fact. When you repeat a preference Copus stopped following, Copus turns the fact into a rule. The note on that reply shows the change, such as **Updated project rules**. You can also ask Copus to keep something as a rule, or pick **Rule** when you add a fact yourself.

You can keep a rule at global, project, or session level. Copus lists rules under **Rules** on the **Memory** page and in a project's **Memory** panel, with a count of the rules at that level. When a level is full of rules, Copus asks you which rule to drop before it saves the new rule.

Copus sends every rule to your AI provider with each message. Keep rules for preferences Copus stopped following.

## See what Copus saves during a conversation

When you mention something worth keeping, such as "I always want sources cited," Copus picks the level and saves the fact while it replies. Copus adds a note to the reply that lists each change, such as **Updated global memory** or **Removed from project memory**. When Copus can't save a fact, the note shows **Not saved to global memory** and gives the reason, such as "you deleted this before" or "already remembered".

Each saved change in the note has links:

- **Undo**: Copus reverses the change. When you undo a removal, Copus restores the fact. A removal shows **Undo** only.
- **Edit**: you change the wording, the level, or the type, then select **Save**. The type is **Rule**, a category for a global fact, or **Fact** for a project or session fact.

Before Copus saves a fact, Copus searches your memory. When a fact already covers the same point, Copus updates that fact instead of adding a second fact.

Copus mentions a save in its reply only when you ask Copus directly, as in "remember that…" or "forget that…". The note shows every other save.

## Add a fact yourself

1. Open **About you** in the sidebar, and select **Memory**.
2. Select **Add a fact**.
3. Pick a category, or **Rule**, and type one short sentence.
4. Select **Save**.

To add a fact to a project, open the project and select **Add a fact** in its **Memory** panel. To add a fact to a scheduled task, open the task, select **Memory** in the top bar, and select **Add a fact**. On both pages, you pick **Fact** or **Rule** instead of a category.

To add a session fact, ask Copus to remember it for the conversation. You can also move a fact Copus saved to **Session** with **Edit** on the note Copus adds to its reply.

## Review, edit, and remove facts

Open **About you** in the sidebar, and select **Memory**. The tab shows your global facts, grouped by category. To find a fact, type into **Search memory…**.

Each fact shows its source and date: **Learned** for a fact Copus saved, **You added** for a fact you added, and **Profile** for your name, email, and about-me note. To change a profile fact, open **Settings** > **Profile**.

Hover a fact to show its controls:

- The type menu shows the fact's category, or **Rule**. Select it to switch the fact to **Rule**, or a rule back to a category or **Fact**.
- **Edit fact** (pencil icon): you change the wording, then press Return or click away to save. Press Esc to cancel.
- **Remove fact** (trash icon): Copus removes the fact.

A project's facts sit in the **Memory** panel on the project's page, with the same controls. When you delete a project or a conversation, Copus deletes its facts.

### Stop Copus saving a removed fact again

When you remove a fact, Copus adds its wording to a record of your most recent removals at that level. Copus skips a later save that matches a recorded removal, unless you repeat the fact in a conversation. When Copus compares wording, it ignores capitalization and end punctuation. A reworded version of the fact counts as a new fact.

To bring back a fact you removed, add it yourself with **Add a fact**.

## Clear out stale facts

When your global memory or a project's memory nears its limit, Copus reviews it and suggests changes under **Suggested cleanup** on the **Memory** page or in the project's **Memory** panel. Each suggestion removes, rewords, or combines facts. Copus shows the wording each suggestion replaces.

- **Approve**: Copus applies the suggestion.
- **Dismiss**: Copus drops the suggestion and keeps the facts as they are.

When a fact changes after Copus suggests a cleanup for that fact, Copus drops the suggestion. Copus sends a notification titled **Memory cleanup suggested** when it has suggestions for you.

When a level is full, Copus stops saving facts to that level from conversations, and sends a **Memory cleanup suggested** notification that says a fact could not be saved. Copus still saves facts you add yourself.

Copus suggests cleanup for global and project facts only, and leaves rules as they are. When session memory is full, Copus removes the oldest session fact to make room for the new fact.

## What Copus skips

- **Secrets.** Copus refuses any fact that looks like a password, API key, token, or private key, including a fact you type in. Store credentials in [Secrets](/connections/secrets).
- **Identifying numbers.** Copus refuses a fact that holds a Social Security, passport, immigration case, or card number. Copus saves the rest of the fact without the number, such as "Holds a US passport."
- **Status and progress.** Copus skips what's finished, what comes next, and the state of a task.
- **Details in passing.** Copus skips an address or a birthday you mention in passing. Copus saves the detail when you ask Copus to remember it.
- **What Copus already has.** Copus skips anything your instructions, response style, or files already say.
- **One-off corrections.** Copus skips a correction to the current draft. Copus saves the correction as a fact when you repeat it or say it applies from now on.

## Limitations

- **Session memory has no page.** You see session facts and rules only in the notes on the replies where Copus saved them. To change a session fact, use **Undo** or **Edit** on the note, or ask Copus.
- **You move a fact to another level only from a conversation.** Use **Edit** on the note on the reply where Copus saved the fact. A fact you add on the **Memory** page stays at the level you add it to.
- **Memory goes to your AI provider.** Copus stores your memory on your Mac. Copus sends your facts when a conversation starts, and your rules with every message. See [Your data stays local](/control/your-data-stays-local).
