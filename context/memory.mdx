Memory is the set of facts Piper keeps about you between tasks. Each fact is one short sentence, such as "Prefers concise answers" or "Anjali leads design." Piper reads the facts in scope at the start of a session, and reads them again on your next message whenever they change.

## The three levels

A fact belongs at the narrowest level where it stays true.

- **Global.** Facts true everywhere you work: who you are, the people around you, what you prefer, how you like things done. Every session reads these.
- **Project.** Facts true inside one project: a decision made, a constraint agreed, a choice that shapes that project's work. Only sessions in that project read them.
- **Session.** Facts true for one task and nothing else, like "use US spelling, just for this."

<Note>
  Global memory is a profile. It holds what stays true about you, and you edit it when something changes. A fact that reads like this week's news belongs at a narrower level, or nowhere.
</Note>

## The four categories

Every global fact sits in one of four categories, and the **Memory** page groups them under these headings:

| Category | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| **Personal info** | Who you are, where you are, your contact details |
| **Team** | Teammates, relationships, who does what around you |
| **Preferences** | What you like: formats, tools, how answers should read |
| **Work style** | How you work: how you plan, review, and want things run |

Project and session facts carry no category. They are a plain list.

## How Piper saves a fact

A fact gets into memory two ways.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Say it while you work">
    Mention something durable in a conversation, like "I always want sources cited," and Piper saves it at the level it belongs to. A short note in that reply says which levels the save touched: **Updated global memory**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Or add it yourself">
    In **Settings**, select **Memory**, then **Add a fact**. Pick a category, type one short sentence, and select **Save**. The category picker appears for global facts only, since project facts carry no category.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The first kind of save runs in the background, once the reply is done. Piper compares the new wording with every fact already at that level, so a fact you removed before is not learned back and the same fact is not stored twice. Reworking or dropping the facts around it is a separate matter. Piper proposes those under **Suggested cleanup** and waits for you.

Each row shows when the fact was established. Hover a row for the full date and where it came from: **Learned** for one Piper picked up, **Added by you** for one you typed. A row drawn from your profile says so and carries no controls here, because the [Profile page](/context/instructions-and-profile) owns it. Your name, your email, and your about-me note appear that way, under **Personal info**.

## Reviewing, editing, and removing

Open **Settings**, then **Memory**, and every global fact sits on one page, grouped by category, newest first. Type in **Search memory…** to narrow it. Hover a fact to edit its wording or remove it.

Removing a fact also stops Piper learning it again. Piper remembers the wording you removed and skips a matching fact on a later background save, ignoring capitalization and any full stop at the end. It holds the 50 most recent removals per level, so a fact removed long ago can come back. You can always add one back yourself, since typing a fact in bypasses that check.

A project's own facts sit on that project's page, in the **Memory** panel, with the same add, edit, and remove controls. Session facts have no page. They expire with the task that made them.

## Suggested cleanup

Piper proposes combining, rewording, or removing facts under **Suggested cleanup**, at the top of the same page. Each suggestion shows the wording it would replace and what it would become. Approve one and Piper applies it immediately. Dismiss one and it goes away.

Suggestions start once global memory reaches 50 facts or a project reaches 30, and a notification titled **Memory cleanup suggested** points you at the list. Either level takes 10 more facts past that mark while you work through the suggestions. Past those 10 the level is full, and Piper tells you a fact could not be saved until you clear some.

Session memory works differently: no suggestions, no page, a hard limit of 20 facts. Past 20, a fact you ask Piper to remember pushes out the oldest one, and a fact Piper picked up on its own is dropped.

<Warning>
  Piper never stores a secret value in memory. Text that looks like a credential, an API key, a token, or a private key is refused, and the whole fact is rejected rather than trimmed. Put credentials in [Secrets](/connections/secrets) instead.
</Warning>

## What reads your memory

Memory reaches every session. Piper has no control for choosing a [custom agent](/autonomous/custom-agents) for a task, so every session runs the built-in agent, named **Piper**, and reads your memory alongside your instructions and your response style.

[Chief of Stuff](/autonomous/chief-of-stuff) is the exception. Work it runs on its own reads no memory and writes none, so nothing it prepares changes what Piper remembers about you.

## Four things that shape what Piper knows

Each one comes from a different place, and they are easy to mix up:

- **Memory** holds facts Piper learns as you work, and you review them.
- **[Instructions and profile](/context/instructions-and-profile)** hold standing rules you write yourself.
- **[Response style](/context/response-style)** sets tone, length, and format.
- **[Knowledge Sources](/context/knowledge-sources)** are folders of your own documents Piper can search.
