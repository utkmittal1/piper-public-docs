title: "Set up Piper"
description: "The onboarding Piper runs the first time you open it, what it collects and why each piece matters, and the three AI providers to choose between."
---

The first time you open Piper, it walks you through a short onboarding. Piper introduces itself, asks who you are and what you work on, connects you to an AI provider, and switches on the skills for your work. It takes a couple of minutes, and Piper is ready to run its first task at the end of it.

Two things come out of that. Piper learns who it is working for, and Piper gets something to think with.

## Tell Piper who it is working for

Piper asks for your name, your email address, and a few sentences about your work.

Your name and address are the small ones. Piper calls you by your name and uses your address to recognize what is yours, such as messages addressed to you.

The note about your work matters most. Piper reads it at the start of every session, so it shapes every reply you get. Write what you do, what you spend your week on, and how you want work handed back. Two or three real sentences beat a job title.

<Tip>
Write it the way you would brief a new colleague on their first day. "I run product for the payments team at a mid-size fintech. Most of my week goes to specs, competitor research, and keeping engineering and design pointed at the same thing. I report to a VP who wants the recommendation first." That tells Piper far more than "product manager" does.
</Tip>

Piper keeps these three as facts about you that it cannot edit or drop on its own. Everything else it learns, it can tidy up over time. These change only when you change them, under **Profile** in **Settings**.

## Choose an AI provider

An AI provider is the service that does Piper's thinking. Piper is the assistant you talk to, and the provider is what Piper sends each turn of the conversation to. Until one is set up and ready, Piper cannot run a task at all.

There are three:

- **Claude Code** runs your tasks on Anthropic's Claude Code agent. Sign in with a Claude account.
- **Codex** runs them on OpenAI's Codex agent. Sign in with a ChatGPT account.
- **Bring your own model** calls a model provider's own API with a key you generate at Anthropic, OpenAI, or DeepSeek. There is no sign-in.

Pick whichever you already have. You can set the others up later and switch between them whenever you like. [AI providers](/concepts/ai-providers) covers each one in full, including what the sign-in asks for and where an API key goes.

You can set any of the three up inside onboarding, so Piper can work the moment it ends. Skip past without one and onboarding still finishes, though Piper cannot run a task until you set one up in **Settings**.

## Switch on the skills for your work

Onboarding ends by asking which skill packs you want, and ticks the ones that suit the note you wrote about your work. What you leave on is what Piper carries, and **Settings** holds the same switches afterwards. [Skills](/working/skills) covers what each pack contains.

<Warning>
Piper saves nothing until you reach the end and select **All set**. Close Piper before that and it keeps none of what you typed, so onboarding starts over next time you open it. Your name, your email, and the note about your work cannot be skipped, because Piper cannot work without them.
</Warning>
