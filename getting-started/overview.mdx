Copus is a personal work assistant that runs on your Mac. Copus reads the apps you connect, finds the work waiting for you, and does that work before you ask. It hands each task back as a finished result you review: a document, an analysis, a meeting brief, a reply, or a deck.

You come back to finished work instead of a blank page. Copus works from the apps you connect, learns how you work, and carries a task to the end.

## Who Copus is for

Copus is for people whose jobs span documents, messages, meetings, data, and decisions: product managers, salespeople, marketers, analysts, founders, and anyone who moves between these kinds of work every day. You know what good work looks like, and you would rather review the work than assemble it.

## Copus works before you ask

Most of the work waiting for you arrives in your mail and on your calendar. Someone asks for a comparison, a decision, a document, or your answer, and the request waits until you find time to start it. Copus starts it for you.

Once you connect Google, Copus checks your mail every 15 minutes, reads your upcoming meetings, and decides what needs work from you. Copus then does the work:

- Produces the document, analysis, comparison, or research someone asks you for
- Writes a prep document for an upcoming meeting
- Drafts a reply that needs thought, in your writing style
- Lays out the choices when a decision is yours, with the reply for each choice ready to send
- Suggests follow-ups on conversations that have gone quiet
- Suggests work nobody asked for, such as a summary of where a project stands

Each result waits on the Home page. You review the result, ask for changes, send it, or dismiss it. See [Work Copus starts before you ask](/working/before-you-ask).

## Copus runs the work to the finish

Type a request into the message box on the Home page. Copus asks what it needs to know, plans the steps, does the work, and hands back the result.

Copus does the research, writes the documents, builds the decks, runs the analyses, and drafts the messages. Copus builds the file itself: a Word document you can edit, a PowerPoint deck you can present, a spreadsheet with live formulas, a PDF. Inside the conversation, Copus also shows charts, tables you can sort, and questions you answer by selecting a choice.

For a job too big for one conversation, Copus keeps going across many steps until the job is done. For work you want repeated, Copus runs the task on a schedule you set. See [How Copus works](/concepts/how-copus-works).

## Copus learns how you work

Copus learns who you work with, how you write, and facts about your work. Copus builds this from your mail when you connect Gmail, from your conversations, and from what you send, change, and dismiss. Copus uses what it learns when it decides what to take on and when it writes in your name, and every task you start reads your memory. The longer you work with Copus, the less you spell out.

You see and edit what Copus keeps on the **About you** page. See [What Copus learns about you](/context/what-copus-learns).

## Copus keeps you in control

**Work Copus starts.** Copus does this work without asking you, and leaves every result on the Home page. In this work, Gmail and Google Calendar are read-only, so Copus sends a reply only when you select **Send**, and creates an event only when you select **Create event**.

**Tasks you start.** You set how much room Copus has, task by task, and you can change it while a task runs. A new task starts in **Manual**, where Copus asks you by default before it sends, posts, or changes anything in a connected app, and before it runs a command that changes something on your Mac. Switch a task to **Autopilot** and Copus does these without asking for the rest of the conversation. In every approval mode, Copus asks before it reads a file that holds credentials or works in your own Chrome. See [Approvals and permissions](/control/approvals-and-permissions).

Copus keeps your tasks, files, memory, and what it learns about you on your Mac. Copus grounds its work in real sources, says what it can't find, and asks you when a choice would change the result.

## What Copus works from

You choose which apps, folders, and websites Copus reads.

- **Your apps.** Gmail, Google Calendar, Google Drive, Slack, Notion, Linear, Atlassian, Asana, and GitHub. See [Integrations](/connections/integrations) for the full list.
- **Your documents.** Add a folder as a knowledge source, and Copus searches the files inside it during a task. See [Knowledge sources](/context/knowledge-sources).
- **The web.** Copus reads pages in a browser of its own. When a page needs an account, Copus asks you to sign in, and keeps that sign-in for later tasks. See [Web and sign-ins](/connections/web-and-sign-ins).

## What Copus needs from you

Copus runs every task on an AI provider. Sign in with the Claude or ChatGPT plan you already pay for, or add an API key. Then connect Google, so Copus can learn about you and start the work waiting in your mail and calendar. [Set up Copus](/getting-started/install) covers both steps.

## Limitations

**Copus works only while it's open.** Copus runs tasks only while it's open and your Mac is awake. A mail check or a scheduled task that comes due while Copus is closed runs once when you open Copus again.

**Copus sends each task's content to your AI provider.** Copus sends your AI provider what each task uses: the conversation, the files the task reads, your memory and instructions, and for work Copus starts, the mail it reads. See [Your data stays local](/control/your-data-stays-local).

**Copus starts work from Gmail and Google Calendar only.** Your other connected apps give Copus context while it works.

**Copus makes mistakes.** Read a result before you send it anywhere.
