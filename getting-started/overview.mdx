Piper is a personal work assistant that runs on your Mac. Piper reads the apps you connect, finds the work waiting for you, and does that work before you ask. It hands each task back as a finished result you review: a document, an analysis, a meeting brief, a reply, or a deck.

You come back to finished work instead of a blank page. Piper works from the apps you connect, learns how you work, and carries a task to the end.

## Who Piper is for

Piper is for people whose jobs span documents, messages, meetings, data, and decisions: product managers, salespeople, marketers, analysts, founders, and anyone who moves between these kinds of work every day. You know what good work looks like, and you would rather review the work than assemble it.

## Piper works before you ask

Most of the work waiting for you arrives in your mail and on your calendar. Someone asks for a comparison, a decision, a document, or your answer, and the request waits until you find time to start it. Piper starts it for you.

Once you connect Google, Piper checks your mail every 15 minutes, reads your upcoming meetings, and decides what needs work from you. Piper then does the work:

- Produces the document, analysis, comparison, or research someone asks you for
- Writes a prep document for an upcoming meeting
- Drafts a reply that needs thought, in your writing style
- Lays out the choices when a decision is yours, with the reply for each choice ready to send
- Suggests follow-ups on conversations that have gone quiet
- Suggests work nobody asked for, such as a summary of where a project stands

Each result waits on the Home page. You review the result, ask for changes, send it, or dismiss it. See [Work Piper starts before you ask](/working/before-you-ask).

## Piper runs the work to the finish

Type a request into the message box on the Home page. Piper asks what it needs to know, plans the steps, does the work, and hands back the result.

Piper does the research, writes the documents, builds the decks, runs the analyses, and drafts the messages. Piper builds the file itself: a Word document you can edit, a PowerPoint deck you can present, a spreadsheet with live formulas, a PDF. Inside the conversation, Piper also shows charts, tables you can sort, and questions you answer by selecting a choice.

For a job too big for one conversation, Piper keeps going across many steps until the job is done. For work you want repeated, Piper runs the task on a schedule you set. See [How Piper works](/concepts/how-piper-works).

## Piper learns how you work

Piper learns who you work with, how you write, and facts about your work. Piper builds this from your mail when you connect Gmail, from your conversations, and from what you send, change, and dismiss. Piper uses what it learns when it decides what to take on and when it writes in your name, and every task you start reads your memory. The longer you work with Piper, the less you spell out.

You see and edit what Piper keeps on the **About you** page. See [What Piper learns about you](/context/what-piper-learns).

## Piper keeps you in control

**Work Piper starts.** Piper does this work without asking you, and leaves every result on the Home page. In this work, Gmail and Google Calendar are read-only, so Piper sends a reply only when you select **Send**, and creates an event only when you select **Create event**.

**Tasks you start.** You set how much room Piper has, task by task, and you can change it while a task runs. A new task starts in **Manual**, where Piper asks you before it sends, posts, or changes anything in a connected app, and before it runs a command that changes something on your Mac. Switch a task to **Autopilot** and Piper does these without asking for the rest of the conversation. In every approval mode, Piper asks before it reads a file that holds credentials or works in your own Chrome. See [Approvals and permissions](/control/approvals-and-permissions).

Piper keeps your tasks, files, memory, and what it learns about you on your Mac. Piper grounds its work in real sources, says what it can't find, and asks you when a choice would change the result.

## What Piper works from

You choose which apps, folders, and websites Piper reads.

- **Your apps.** Gmail, Google Calendar, Google Drive, Slack, Notion, Linear, Atlassian, Asana, and GitHub. See [Integrations](/connections/integrations) for the full list.
- **Your documents.** Add a folder as a knowledge source, and Piper searches the files inside it during a task. See [Knowledge sources](/context/knowledge-sources).
- **The web.** Piper reads pages in a browser of its own. When a page needs an account, Piper asks you to sign in, and keeps that sign-in for later tasks. See [Web and sign-ins](/connections/web-and-sign-ins).

## What Piper needs from you

Piper runs every task on an AI provider. Sign in with the Claude or ChatGPT plan you already pay for, or add an API key. Then connect Google, so Piper can learn about you and start the work waiting in your mail and calendar. [Set up Piper](/getting-started/install) covers both steps.

## Limitations

**Piper works only while it's open.** Piper runs tasks only while it's open and your Mac is awake. A mail check or a scheduled task that comes due while Piper is closed runs once when you open Piper again.

**Piper sends each task's content to your AI provider.** Piper sends your AI provider what each task uses: the conversation, the files the task reads, your memory and instructions, and for work Piper starts, the mail it reads. See [Your data stays local](/control/your-data-stays-local).

**Piper starts work from Gmail and Google Calendar only.** Your other connected apps give Piper context while it works.

**Piper makes mistakes.** Read a result before you send it anywhere.
