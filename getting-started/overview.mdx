Piper is a personal AI assistant for work. It gets ahead of what you need, runs the work in the background, and hands each task back as a finished result you can review.

You come back to a draft, a brief, a deck, or an analysis rather than a blank page. Piper works from the tools you connect, remembers how you work, and drives a task to the end instead of stopping every few minutes to ask.

## Who Piper is for

Piper is for people whose jobs span documents, messages, meetings, data, and decisions. Product managers, salespeople, marketers, analysts, founders, and anyone whose work changes shape through the day. You know what good work looks like, and you would rather review it than assemble it.

You never write code, and no page here sends you to a terminal or a setting meant for engineers.

## Piper works before you ask

You choose which of your connected tools Piper watches. Within those, it looks ahead: the reply you still owe, the meeting that needs prep, the risk buried in a long thread, the document that will be due. It starts the work and brings you the result.

Nothing it prepares this way goes out on its own. A draft waits for you to read it and send it. See [Chief of Stuff](/autonomous/chief-of-stuff).

## Piper learns how you work

Piper reads what you tell it about yourself, and keeps what it learns as you work: the decisions you made, the corrections you gave, the way you like a thing written. It works from that rather than asking you to repeat yourself, so the longer you work together, the less you spell out.

What it keeps is yours to read, edit, or delete. See [Memory](/context/memory).

## Piper runs the work to the finish

Piper does the research, writes the documents, builds the decks, runs the analyses, prepares the briefs, and drafts the replies. It builds the real file, not an outline of one.

A big job keeps going for hours across many sessions. A routine one repeats on a schedule you agree. See [How Piper works](/concepts/how-piper-works).

## Piper keeps you in control

You set how much room Piper has, session by session, from approving each action to letting it run on its own. You can change that at any point, including while a task is running.

A new session starts on **Autopilot**, the wider of the two settings, so Piper acts without checking with you first, sending through a connected app included. Switch the session to **Manual** whenever an outbound action would matter. See [Approvals and permissions](/control/approvals-and-permissions).

Piper grounds what it says in real sources, names what it could not find, and asks when a choice would change the result. Your sessions, files, and memory stay in a store on your own Mac.

## What Piper needs from you

An AI provider, the service that does Piper's thinking. Piper cannot run a task until you set one up, and [Set up Piper](/getting-started/install) covers the choice.

Everything after that widens what Piper can reach rather than switching it on: the apps you connect, the folders of documents you point it at, and the standing instructions you write.

## The honest limits

**Piper works while it is open.** You can walk away and Piper keeps going, as long as Piper is running and your Mac is on and awake. Quit Piper or let the machine sleep and the work stops until you are back.

**Your work stays on this Mac. Your requests do not.** Piper keeps your sessions, files, and memory locally, and each turn of the conversation goes out to the AI provider you chose. See [Your data stays local](/control/your-data-stays-local).

**Piper can be wrong.** It names its sources where it can, and it still gets things wrong. Read the result before you send it anywhere.
